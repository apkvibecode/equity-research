01Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Two engines: a cash-generative combustibles business in structural decline, and a reduced-risk portfolio management hopes will replace it.

Combustibles — The Cash Engine

  • Brands: Lucky Strike, Dunhill, Camel, Newport — plus Grizzly in US smokeless
  • Economics: Revenue held up by repeated price increases offsetting falling stick volumes; excise-tax pass-through is central to pricing
  • Geography: Global ex-US focus, with a major US presence via Reynolds

Reduced-Risk / New Category

  • Vuse — described in the pack as the top global vaping brand
  • glo — heated tobacco
  • Velo — nicotine pouches
  • Role: Growth and regulatory hedge; still the smaller part of the mix versus combustibles

Structure, Pivots & Supply Chain

Pivot: Shift from pure combustibles toward reduced-risk — the key structural pivot in the pack. Supply chain dependencies: tobacco leaf sourcing, manufacturing footprint, and the ability to pass excise taxes through to consumers without accelerating volume loss. No further segment revenue split was provided in the verified pack, so none is invented here.

03Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Where the moat is real

  • Brand scale — global portfolio across price tiers
  • Distribution — entrenched retail reach, especially via Reynolds in the US
  • Pricing power — historically able to raise prices in declining volumes
  • Vuse leadership — leading position in vaping gives a credible reduced-risk bridge

Where it is fragile

  • Moat protects cash flow, not growth — category volumes are in decline
  • Regulation can impair the moat overnight (flavour / menthol rules, plain packaging, tax)
  • ESG exclusion by many funds limits the buyer base and can keep the multiple low
  • Illicit trade and FX translation (global ex-US earnings) pressure reported results

Pack positioning: brand scale + distribution + pricing power + Vuse leadership. No market-share percentages were verified in the pack, so none are quoted.

04Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts

  • Reduced-risk growth — Vuse / glo / Velo scaling faster than combustibles decline
  • Continued pricing in combustibles without volume acceleration to the downside
  • Cash return — dividend maintained / grown and buybacks as secondary support

Risks

  • Regulation & tax: excise rises, marketing restrictions
  • Menthol bans — direct hit to Newport / US combustibles
  • Litigation — ongoing tobacco liability overhang
  • Volume decline accelerating beyond pricing power
  • FX — global earnings translated back to reporting currency
  • ESG / financing: higher cost of capital, excluded mandates

Intrinsic value — British American Tobacco (BTI)

Price (30 Oct 2026 close): $61.52. Peter Lynch fair value: $20.40. Discount/premium: +201.6% — i.e. the shares are trading +201.6% vs Lynch fair value. Discount/premium % = (Price − FV) / FV × 100; negative = discount, positive = premium. Upside to fair value (secondary figure): −66.8%, computed as (FV − Price) / Price × 100.

Method: Peter Lynch fair value = earnings growth rate × TTM diluted EPS. The growth rate is the arithmetic average of the 5 annual year-over-year net-income growth rates; the 5% floor / 25% cap is applied AFTER averaging.

Caution — read before using this figure: the average is not economically meaningful. The 2023 loss (−£14.37bn) reflects the large US combustibles impairment, and year-over-year percentages computed across a negative base (2023 and 2024) are arithmetical artefacts, not growth rates. The method's 5% floor is applied mechanically to that average, so $20.40 is a floor-driven mechanical output, not an estimate of underlying earnings power.
