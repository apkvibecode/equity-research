Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Two segments, one franchise

Goldman Sachs now runs as a focused institutional firm with two reporting segments:

  • Global Banking & Markets — advisory and underwriting plus FICC and equities trading. This is the earnings engine and the source of most of the volatility: Q3 2026 equities trading revenue was $6.4B, up 72% year over year, the standout line of the quarter.
  • Asset & Wealth Management — asset management (including a growing alternatives book) and private wealth management. This is the steadier, fee-based side the firm is trying to grow so earnings depend less on trading conditions.

Structural pivot: the retreat from consumer banking is now largely complete, ending the multi-year experiment with mass-market consumer products and refocusing capital and management attention on the institutional and wealth franchises where Goldman holds elite positions.

Q3 2026 headline (confirmed against Goldman's own release): revenue $18.68B, net income $5.29B, EPS $17.01.

How to read a bank balance sheet here

Debt-to-equity and EV/EBITDA — useful for industrial companies — mislead for a bank, whose balance sheet is the business: deposits and trading inventories are raw material, not distress. The diagnostics that matter for GS are regulatory capital ratios, ROE through the cycle, and the mix shift toward fee-based Asset & Wealth Management revenue, which deserves a higher multiple than trading revenue because it does not disappear in a quiet quarter. Where this pass could not verify a figure (forward multiples, Q3 ROE, dividend yield, buyback totals), this report says so instead of filling the gap with an estimate dressed as fact.

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

  • Premier IB/trading franchise. Goldman sits at the top table of global M&A advisory, underwriting and institutional trading. Boardrooms and sovereign clients call Goldman (and JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley) first — that default-shortlist position is the moat, and it compounds: league-table rank wins the next mandate.
  • Elite talent and client access. The firm's partnership culture and pay-for-performance model attract the bankers and traders whose personal client relationships drive deal flow. The flip side of that strength is the compensation-ratio risk in Section 4.
  • Asset-management scale. A large alternatives and wealth platform gives GS a second, less cyclical earnings leg and cross-selling into the same client base it advises and trades for.
  • Barriers to entry are regulatory and reputational: global trading requires enormous capital, licences in every major market, and decades of counterparty trust. No new entrant replicates this; the realistic competition is the existing bulge bracket — JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citi — plus elite boutiques in advisory.
  • Pricing power shows up in trading spreads and advisory fees at the top of the market: clients pay for certainty of execution on the largest, most complex transactions, where a failed deal costs far more than the fee difference.
04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts

  • IB/trading super-cycle: Q3's equities trading print (+72%) shows the upside when deal-making and market activity run hot together; a sustained cycle lifts both segments at once.
  • Alternatives growth: continued fundraising and deployment in private markets grows the high-margin, recurring-fee revenue the market values most.
  • Mix shift re-rating: as Asset & Wealth Management becomes a larger share of earnings, the market can justify paying a higher multiple for the whole firm.

Downside risks

  • Highest cyclicality in this universe: of the financials covered in this series, GS earnings swing the most with markets — a deal drought or a quiet trading year hits GS first and hardest.
  • Trading-revenue volatility: a $6.4B equities quarter can be followed by a far weaker one; annualising a record quarter is exactly the error the verification pass corrected in the revenue maths.
  • Compensation ratio: if pay absorbs the trading windfall, revenue records do not convert into EPS records.
  • Regulatory capital: higher capital requirements or stress-test outcomes can cap buybacks and dividends regardless of earnings.

AI trade positioning — Goldman Sachs (GS)

Role: Beneficiary. While the AI trade runs, Goldman is close to the centre of the trade without being an AI company: it advises on AI deals, underwrites AI financings and trades AI names — Q3 2026 equities trading rose 72% to $6.4bn (pack) in what the pack calls an IB/trading super-cycle — and its alternatives business can raise AI-infrastructure capital.

While the AI trade runs — multiple channel: The multiple channel is modest in level (~12x trailing) but high in beta: Goldman’s earnings, and therefore its market value, swing with deal and trading volumes, so an AI unwind hits earnings before it hits any AI premium.

When the AI trade unwinds: If the AI trade unwinds, what breaks in order is: (1) demand — deal announcements, underwriting and trading volumes collapse first, as they did in past cycles; (2) multiple — compression from ~12x toward a single-digit through-cycle level for a cyclical broker; (3) balance sheet — compensation and capital flexibility tighten, though the pack gives no AI-capex debt figure for Goldman. Triggers to watch: global M&A volumes, equities-trading revenue, and any AI-financing or data-centre deal cancellations.
