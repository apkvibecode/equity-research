01

Business Model & Segment Breakdown

What NEM sells, and where the ounces come from

Newmont is described in the research pack as the world's leading gold miner, producing gold as its core product with copper, silver, zinc and lead as additional metals. That mix matters: gold drives the investment case, while the base- and by-product metals — copper in particular — provide a partial offset when gold is soft and an extra earnings lever when it is not.

Attributable gold production was 5.89 million ounces in FY2025 (Motley Fool, via the pack). The current asset base is the product of two transformational deals: the Goldcorp acquisition in 2019 and the Newcrest acquisition in 2023. The Newcrest deal is still the live structural story — the pack flags portfolio pruning post-Newcrest as an ongoing item, meaning non-core asset sales and integration remain part of the near-term narrative rather than settled history.

Revenue streams

  • Gold — core: 5.89m oz attributable production in FY2025; the dominant revenue and valuation driver.
  • Copper — by-product / co-product: named in the pack as a distinct catalyst; cushions gold-price downside at the margin.
  • Silver, zinc, lead: additional metals in the production mix; smaller contributors, not broken out separately in the verified pack.

Structure & footprint

  • Built by acquisition: Goldcorp (2019) and Newcrest (2023) assembled today's scale.
  • Diversified jurisdictions: the pack cites geographic diversification across mining jurisdictions as a core strength — no single-country concentration figure is carried in the verified pack, so no percentage split is shown here.
  • Post-deal pruning: portfolio simplification after Newcrest is an active programme, per the pack.

A segment-level revenue split and a geographic revenue split are OPEN QUESTION — the verified research pack does not carry them for NEM, so this note does not present a breakdown it cannot source. The honest summary the pack supports is: one dominant product (gold), one meaningful secondary metal (copper), and a portfolio still being reshaped after a major acquisition.

Reading the multiple. At 11.9× trailing earnings and 4.3× sales, NEM screens cheap against the broader market — but for a gold miner that cheapness is partly an artefact of where gold sits in its cycle. When the gold price is high, TTM EPS is flattered and the P/E mechanically compresses; the same multiple at a cyclical gold low would look very different. The pack's valuation work (§5) therefore leans on price scenarios for gold rather than on multiple expansion.

03

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Scale and cost position — not pricing power

The pack is explicit about the nature of the moat, and it is worth quoting its logic rather than dressing it up: gold is a commodity and Newmont is a price-taker — the moat is cost position, not pricing power. Newmont cannot charge more for an ounce than the market price; what it can do is produce that ounce more cheaply, more reliably, and for longer than smaller rivals.

Where the advantage sits

  • Scale: world's leading gold miner, 5.89m oz attributable in FY2025 — purchasing, technical and financing advantages follow from being the largest operator.
  • Reserve base: cited by the pack as a core moat element — long-life reserves underpin production visibility that smaller miners cannot match.
  • Diversified jurisdictions: production spread across jurisdictions reduces single-asset and single-country disruption risk.
  • Metal mix: copper and other by-products diversify revenue away from a pure single-metal exposure.

Where it does not

  • No pricing power: revenue per ounce is set by the gold market, full stop.
  • Cost inflation exposure: labour, energy and consumables inflation directly compresses the cost advantage — flagged as a risk in §4.
  • Execution risk at scale: a portfolio this large, reshaped by two major acquisitions, carries mine-level execution risk that a focused single-asset miner does not.
  • Depletion: every ounce mined must eventually be replaced — reserve replacement is a permanent capital requirement of the model.

Positioning takeaway: within gold mining, NEM is the scale-and-longevity choice — the name an investor holds for diversified, reserve-backed gold exposure rather than for torque to a single discovery or mine. That positioning supports a core-but-modest portfolio weight (§6), not a concentrated bet: the return driver remains the gold price, which Newmont does not control.

04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

One dominant variable, several second-order ones

Catalysts

  • Gold price: the primary catalyst in both directions — sustained strength flows almost directly to earnings at NEM's margin structure.
  • Copper by-product: copper contribution can support earnings and cash flow independently of gold.
  • Portfolio pruning post-Newcrest: selling non-core assets after the 2023 deal can simplify the portfolio and return focus (and potentially capital) to the core mines.
  • Cost position: any demonstrated cost discipline widens the miner's one real moat (§3).

Downside risks

  • Gold correction: the mirror image of the main catalyst — a gold pullback compresses the flattered TTM earnings behind the 11.9× P/E and the multiple can de-rate at the same time.
  • Cost inflation: mining input inflation erodes margins even if gold holds flat.
  • Mine execution: operational underperformance at major assets, or integration slippage in the post-Newcrest portfolio.
  • Jurisdiction risk: diversification mitigates but does not eliminate political, permitting and fiscal risk across mining jurisdictions.

Note the asymmetry the pack's own scenarios imply (§5): the 12–18-month bear case is $88, about −17% from $106.61, against a bull case of $140, about +31%. That spread is mostly a statement about gold, not about Newmont-specific execution — which is exactly why position size, not conviction, is the right risk control here.

Recommendation & Portfolio Sizing

Research views — not guarantees, not investment advice

Short term (12–18 months) — Buy

The pack's recommendation is Buy for the short term: an 11.9× trailing multiple with a base target of $118 (+11%) and a bull case of $140 gives a favourable near-term skew, provided the holder accepts that the position is, in substance, a gold-price position. The bear case (−17% to $88) is the cost of that exposure being wrong.

Long term (5 years) — Hold / Buy

The pack's recommendation is Hold/Buy for the long term. The 5-year weighted modelled estimate of ~$123 is only ~+15% over five years, so the long-term case rests on NEM's role as diversified, reserve-backed gold exposure — a portfolio diversifier — rather than on compounding growth. Existing holders can hold; new money should size it as a diversifier, not a growth engine.

Position-size calculator — 1.5% target weight

Suggested weight for a mid-30s investor at risk score 8/10: 1.5% of total portfolio. A deliberately small sleeve: large enough to give real gold diversification, small enough that a gold correction (bear case −17% on the position ≈ −0.26% at portfolio level) cannot dominate the outcome. Rationale: NEM's return driver is a commodity price outside the company's control, so it earns a diversifier's weight, not a core holding's.

AI trade positioning — Newmont (NEM)

Role: Low exposure. Gold has no AI input channel: Newmont's earnings are set by the gold price, real rates and mine costs, and AI sentiment reaches it only through general risk appetite and the dollar.

While the AI trade runs — demand channel: While the AI trade runs, demand barely moves: gold demand comes from investors, jewellers and central banks, and AI capex consumes no material gold.

While the AI trade runs — multiple channel: On multiples, there is no AI premium to unwind (trailing P/E 11.9 in the pack is a gold-price multiple), so compression would come from a falling gold price, not AI sentiment.

When the AI trade unwinds, what breaks in order: (1) demand — gold-price weakness as risk appetite returns; (2) multiple compression toward a pre-AI/through-cycle multiple; (3) balance sheet — On the balance sheet, the position is defensive: both miners carry net cash (~$3.4bn each in this build) and no AI-capex debt, so an unwind hits through the gold price, not solvency.

Specific trigger(s) to watch: Triggers to watch: a sharp gold-price correction on rising real rates, a risk-on rotation out of haven assets, and cost inflation at the mines themselves. No quantified AI sensitivity is claimed (no pack figure supports one). Key distinction: commodity prices, not AI sentiment, drive this name, so an AI unwind hits it mainly through the commodity cycle and risk appetite.
