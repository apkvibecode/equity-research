01

Business Model & Segment Breakdown

AbbVie is a research-based biopharmaceutical company organised into four portfolios: Immunology, Neuroscience, Oncology and Aesthetics. It sells branded prescription drugs — pricing power comes from patent protection and prescriber habit, and revenue ends abruptly when patents do, which is why the Humira story (once the world's best-selling drug, now in steep biosimilar decline) frames everything else.

Q3 2026 (reported 30 Oct 2026): revenue $17.40B, up 10.3% reported (+10.5% operational). Adjusted EPS $3.82, beating the $3.74 consensus. Management raised FY26 adjusted EPS guidance to $13.95–13.99 (from $13.76–13.96) on revenue of about $67.8B. The Apogee Therapeutics acquisition was completed in Q3, adding immunology pipeline depth.

Geography & structure

The business is US-heavy where it matters most: Skyrizi's Q3 sales split $5.02B US / $0.76B ex-US — about 87% of the lead drug's revenue is American, which concentrates both pricing power and US policy risk (IRA negotiation, MFN/Medicaid pricing) in one market.

Structural pivot: AbbVie has now fully rotated from Humira dependence to the Skyrizi/Rinvoq immunology pair, and is buying pipeline (Apogee, completed Q3) rather than building every asset internally.

03

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

What protects it

  • Patent estate: Skyrizi composition-of-matter protection runs to 2033 (per AbbVie's 10-K, as reported by secondary analysis); Rinvoq US generic entry is reported settled to ~2037. That is an unusually long runway for the two growth drugs.
  • Prescriber base: deep immunology relationships across dermatology, gastroenterology and rheumatology — switching a stable patient is clinically and administratively costly.
  • Brand franchises outside immunology: Botox (therapeutic and cosmetic) is a two-decade brand with clinic-level lock-in.
  • Scale in manufacturing & payer negotiation that smaller immunology rivals cannot match.

Who attacks it

  • Johnson & Johnson — Stelara and Tremfya compete directly in psoriasis and IBD.
  • Eli Lilly — expanding immunology presence alongside its metabolic franchise.
  • Novartis and UCB — established immunology portfolios (Cosentyx; Bimzelx).
  • JAK-class rivals — Rinvoq competes within its own drug class on safety labelling as much as efficacy.

Pricing power is real but politically capped: as a top US-revenue drugmaker, AbbVie sits squarely in the path of IRA price negotiation and MFN/Medicaid pricing proposals.

04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts

  • Skyrizi (+22.7%) and Rinvoq (+23.9%) still compounding above 20% — every quarter that holds pushes the "post-Humira cliff" narrative further out.
  • ABBV-466, a Skyrizi-combination programme, in Phase 2a — a lifecycle-extension path for the flagship.
  • Apogee Therapeutics pipeline, acquired and closed in Q3, adds immunology shots on goal.
  • Dividend growth: +5.2% announced for 2027 ($1.82/quarter) supports the income-holder base.
  • FY26 guidance already raised once (adj. EPS $13.95–13.99); further raises would re-rate the stock toward the bull case.

Downside risks

  • Concentration: Skyrizi + Rinvoq = 48.7% of Q3 revenue. One safety signal or payer decision hits half the company.
  • The 2031–33 patent wave: Evaluate's forecast puts ~$50B of one-year AbbVie revenue exposure in 2032 — the largest single-company cliff in its forecast.
  • Imbruvica −26% on IRA pricing and competition — a live example of policy directly removing revenue.
  • Leverage: $64.32B net debt limits flexibility if growth disappoints.
  • Aesthetics softness: −1.4% in Q3; consumer-facing and rate-sensitive.
  • MFN / Medicaid pricing proposals aimed at exactly AbbVie's US-heavy revenue mix.

ABBV — AI trade positioning

Role: Low exposure. AI is a research-productivity tool for this company, not a product it sells; the pack's thesis rests on its drug/device franchise, with no AI revenue line disclosed.

While the AI trade runs. Demand channel: AI in immunology/oncology discovery and in commercial targeting for Skyrizi/Rinvoq, supporting — not replacing — the post-Humira franchise transition. Multiple channel: ABBV's 18.80× forward P/E in the pack is a post-patent-cliff pharma multiple, with no AI premium to give back.

When the AI trade unwinds — what breaks, in order. First, demand: little breaks on AI capex — demand follows prescriptions, payer coverage and patent cliffs, so an AI unwind reaches this name only via a general de-rating. Second, multiple compression: low — the pack forward P/E (high-teens to low-30s for these names) is driven by earnings/pipeline, and an unwind removes at most a small sentiment premium. Third, balance sheet: low direct AI-capex debt; the real risks are the pack's (patent cliffs, litigation, payer pressure), not AI inventory. Trigger to watch: a Skyrizi/Rinvoq competitive or pricing setback, or a renewed Humira-biosimilar erosion, rather than AI spending.
