How the normalisation works, so the arithmetic is checkable: effective weight = published weight × verdict factor. Buy-family verdicts (Buy, Buy-satellite, Buy-selective, Buy-speculative, Buy-on-weakness, Buy-access-dependent, Buy-small) count at 1.0; Hold-family verdicts (Hold and all Hold-… variants) count at 0.5; Sell / Sell-Trim counts at 0. The 12–18-month spread uses each name's short verdict; the 5-year spread uses its long verdict (a Sell long verdict — only CUAEF — counts at 0). Each name's normalised % is its effective weight divided by that horizon's total effective weight, times 100. Names published at 0% (2222, CNOOC, CUAEF) stay at 0% in both horizons and are shown, not dropped. Never stack the sector sleeves: they were sized independently.

Intrinsic value — DCF only (revised update: methodology & caveats)
  • What changed in this DCF-only revision: intrinsic value is now DCF only. The DDM (Gordon), asset-based (BVPS floor) and weighted-average figures from the November build have been removed from every ticker report and from this index; DCF inputs and assumptions are unchanged. The index columns are now DCF intrinsic and Disc/Prem % vs DCF. Price reference throughout: 30 Oct 2026 close.
  • Discount/premium convention (used everywhere, no exceptions): Disc/Prem % = (Price − DCF intrinsic) / DCF intrinsic × 100, recomputed from the rounded DCF and price shown in each report so a reader can reproduce it. Negative = trading at a DISCOUNT to DCF intrinsic (price below intrinsic); positive = at a PREMIUM. The index column shows the DCF-intrinsic figure; each ticker report shows its DCF inputs and assumptions in full.
  • Method — DCF (unchanged from the November build): 10-year projection of free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex; TTM FCF normalised where the pack flags it as peak/distorted) at a CONSTANT growth rate per ticker from pack evidence (capped at 15%), discounted at r = 10% default, 8.5% for regulated utilities and REITs, 12% for high-risk/speculative names. Terminal value by Gordon growth on year-10 cash flow at terminal g = 3% (2.5% utilities/REITs). Equity value = PV of projected cash flow + net cash, divided by diluted shares — EXCEPT utilities (see restatement note below) and the withdrawn/no-DCF names below. Banks/insurers use a distributable-earnings proxy (net income to common after required capital retention), never bank operating cash flow; REITs use AFFO-based cash flow (FFO where AFFO is not given, labelled as such); loss-making names use a forward normalised FCF assumption labelled HIGHLY UNCERTAIN.
  • 5-yr CAGR and % to wtd (new columns, both tables, immediately after the 5-yr weighted column): % to wtd = (5-yr weighted − Price) / Price × 100; 5-yr CAGR = (5-yr weighted / Price)^(1/5) − 1, as a % — the compound annual growth rate implied if the price moved from the 30 Oct 2026 close to the 5-year scenario weighted price over 5 years. Both are computed from each row’s existing price and 5-yr weighted values (SNDK uses split-adjusted figures: price ~$143.86, weighted ~$127). Non-numeric weighteds (“see artifact”, “—”, qualitative-only) and unparseable row prices render as “N/A” for both columns. The 5-yr weighted is the v1 scenario weighted price, NOT the intrinsic value — it is a different figure from the DCF intrinsic column and must not be read as one.
  • Utilities — DCF RESTATED (correction applied, still in force): the first build subtracted net debt from a regulated-earnings proxy that is already after interest, double-counting debt. In this corrected build the utilities DCF excludes the net-debt term entirely (PV of projected cash flow + PV of terminal value, ÷ shares). On the DCF-only basis the utilities now screen at discounts: EXC −25.5%, NEE −19.1%, XEL −16.8% (vs DCF intrinsic).
  • Withdrawn DCFs — DE and ORCL; no DCF — SFTBY: DE’s DCF ($11.29 memo) is withdrawn (consolidated FCF/debt include Deere Financial while ~$49B of financing receivables are excluded — a distortion dwarfing the figure); ORCL’s DCF ($99 memo) is likewise withdrawn (its $15B base assumes capex moderating to ~$32B against $90–95B FY2027 guidance). Both withdrawn figures remain visible in their reports, labelled WITHDRAWN with the reason, and are never presented as an intrinsic value; their index cells read “N/A”. SFTBY has no DCF available (investment holding company; consolidated FCF is dominated by investment flows and NAV, not FCF, is the right lens), so its index cells also read “N/A”.
  • Extreme values carry their own caveats in their reports — do not quote them bare: BE’s DCF excludes its ~$6B product backlog and ~$20B service agreements (only $0.72B was binding RPO at 30 Sep 2026; the service book converts over 5–20 years and is annually terminable); TSLA’s DCF ($23) reflects TTM FCF depressed by AI capex; WMT/COST DCF values ($21 / $476) reflect small FCF bases against very high multiples at a 10% discount rate (their FCF bases were confirmed exactly on re-sourcing).
  • Currency and date caveats: 2222 intrinsics are in SAR and its price (SAR 25.92) is a 2 Oct 2026 snapshot, NOT a verified 30 Oct close; CNOOC intrinsics are in CNY with the same date caveat. SNDK is split-adjusted throughout (8-for-1 split payable ~19 Nov 2026; adjusted price ~$143.86, adjusted 5-yr weighted ~$127). Thin OTC prints (PCCYF, CUAEF, GMBXF, AIQUF, ZIJMF and others flagged in v1) remain indicative only.
  • Customers: B2B tables list the top 7 customers by revenue importance where disclosed, otherwise the largest known customers with ranking explicitly not disclosed; revenue shares appear only where disclosed. A customer’s DCF intrinsic and discount/premium vs DCF are shown only if that customer is itself one of the 124 covered tickers (taken from this same build); private or uncovered customers read “not in coverage — no intrinsic on file”; withdrawn/no-DCF customers (ORCL, DE, SFTBY) read “N/A (DCF withdrawn)” / “N/A (no DCF)”. The read-through thesis (undervalued customers as a potential tailwind) is stated with its caveat in each report: an undervalued customer does not guarantee higher spending.
  • Unchanged by this revision: verdicts, scenario prices (including all 5-yr weighted prices) and published weights are UNCHANGED. A large premium vs DCF intrinsic is a statement about what the uniform discount rate implies, not a new sell call.
  • Verification: in this DCF-only revision every Disc/Prem % was recomputed from the rounded DCF and 30 Oct price shown, every customer DCF figure was recomputed from the same master data, and the 5-yr CAGR / % to wtd columns were checked against worked examples (NVDA: +21.8% CAGR, +167.8% to wtd; SNDK adjusted: −2.5% CAGR, −11.7% to wtd). All intrinsic figures remain MODELLED ESTIMATES from stated inputs and assumptions, not verified facts and not investment advice. Inputs that could not be re-sourced are labelled UNVERIFIED/estimate in their reports, never presented as fact.

Glossary

Every term used in the tables above and in the linked reports.

ADR
American Depositary Receipt — a US-listed certificate representing shares of a foreign company (e.g. TSM, TM), so the price is in USD but the underlying trades abroad.
AFFO
Adjusted Funds From Operations — FFO further adjusted for recurring capital spending and straight-line rent; the closer REIT analogue of free cash flow.
Bear / Base / Bull case
The pessimistic / central / optimistic scenario in a valuation. Each carries explicit assumptions and a probability (scenario weight); none is a promise.
Buyback
A company repurchasing its own shares, which reduces the share count and lifts per-share figures; part of capital allocation alongside dividends and reinvestment.
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate — the smoothed yearly growth rate between two values over a period.
Consensus
The average of sell-side analyst estimates or price targets. “Consensus only” means the source report carries the consensus anchor and no modelled Bull/Bear split.
D/E (debt-to-equity)
Total debt divided by shareholders' equity — a leverage gauge. Higher means more of the business is funded by borrowing.
Data-centre pipeline
The contracted and planned capacity (usually in MW/GW) a utility, REIT or developer has agreed or announced to build for data-centre customers — a forward growth indicator.
Dividend yield
Annual dividend per share divided by the share price — the income return before any price movement.
EPS
Earnings per share — net income divided by the share count. GAAP EPS includes one-offs; non-GAAP/underlying EPS strips them out.
EV/EBITDA
Enterprise value (market cap plus net debt) divided by earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a capital-structure-neutral valuation multiple.
FCF (free cash flow)
Operating cash flow minus capital expenditure — the cash left for dividends, buybacks, debt repayment or reinvestment.
FFO
Funds From Operations — the REIT earnings measure: net income plus depreciation/amortisation of real estate, excluding property sale gains.
Frozen-parameter model
The 5-year projection method used in this series: today's net margin, P/E and share count are held (“frozen”) unless a case changes them, and only revenue growth varies by scenario.
LOE (loss of exclusivity)
The date a drug's patent protection ends and generic/biosimilar competition can enter — a key pharma downside risk (relevant to MRK, PFE, ABBV).
MFN (most favoured nation)
In this series, US drug-pricing policy pressure to match the lowest price paid in comparable countries — a margin risk for large pharma names.
Merger arbitrage
Trading a stock on the spread between its market price and an agreed takeover price; the return depends on the deal closing, not on standalone fundamentals (see D).
NAV discount
When a holding company or conglomerate trades below the estimated net asset value of what it owns — the gap is the discount (relevant to SFTBY, BRK.B, TCEHY).
Net margin
Net income divided by revenue — how much of each sales dollar survives as profit.
OTC
Over-the-counter — traded off a major exchange, often thinly, so prices are indicative and sometimes unverified (many foreign names here are OTC ADRs).
P/AFFO
Price divided by AFFO per share — the REIT multiple that pairs with FFO-based valuation instead of P/E.
P/E — trailing
Price divided by the last twelve months' actual EPS. Meaningless or misleading when earnings are depressed or one-off-heavy.
P/E — forward
Price divided by expected EPS over the next twelve months — depends on analyst estimates being right.
P/FFO
Price divided by FFO per share — the standard REIT valuation multiple, used in this series instead of trailing P/E for real estate.
P/S (price-to-sales)
Market cap divided by revenue — useful when earnings are thin or negative, but it ignores profitability entirely.
Rate base
The asset base on which a regulated utility is allowed to earn a regulated return; growing the rate base (e.g. grid and data-centre build-out) grows allowed earnings.
REIT
Real Estate Investment Trust — a property company that must pay out most taxable income as dividends and is valued on FFO/AFFO rather than EPS.
ROE (return on equity)
Net income divided by shareholders' equity — how profitably a company uses its owners' capital.
Scenario weights
The probabilities attached to Bear / Base / Bull cases (commonly 25% / 50% / 25% in this series) used to blend them into a weighted price.
SHOP (senior housing operating portfolio)
The segment of a healthcare REIT (e.g. WELL, VTR) where the REIT shares in the operating economics of senior housing, rather than only collecting rent.
Triple-net (NNN)
A lease where the tenant pays rent plus taxes, insurance and maintenance — the landlord's income is comparatively passive and predictable (e.g. O).
Verdict factor
The normalisation rule in this index: Buy-family = 1.0, Hold-family = 0.5, Sell = 0, applied to a published weight to get its effective weight for a horizon.
Weighted price
The probability-weighted blend of the Bear, Base and Bull scenario prices — read as “the maths under these assumptions”, not a prediction.
