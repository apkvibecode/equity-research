How the normalisation works, so the arithmetic is checkable: effective weight = published weight × verdict factor. Buy-family verdicts (Buy, Buy-satellite, Buy-selective, Buy-speculative, Buy-on-weakness, Buy-access-dependent, Buy-small) count at 1.0; Hold-family verdicts (Hold and all Hold-… variants) count at 0.5; Sell / Sell-Trim counts at 0. The 12–18-month spread uses each name's short verdict; the 5-year spread uses its long verdict (a Sell long verdict — only CUAEF — counts at 0). Each name's normalised % is its effective weight divided by that horizon's total effective weight, times 100. Names published at 0% (2222, CNOOC, CUAEF) stay at 0% in both horizons and are shown, not dropped. Never stack the sector sleeves: they were sized independently.

Intrinsic value — Peter Lynch fair value (methodology & caveats)
  • What changed in this revision: the single intrinsic value is now the Peter Lynch fair value. The DCF figures, DCF inputs and withdrawn-DCF notes from the previous revision have been removed from every ticker report and from this index; the DDM (Gordon), asset-based and weighted-average figures were already removed in an earlier revision. The index columns are now Peter Lynch fair value and Disc/Prem % vs Lynch FV. Price reference throughout: 30 Oct 2026 close.
  • Discount/premium convention (used everywhere, no exceptions): Disc/Prem % = (Price − Peter Lynch fair value) / Peter Lynch fair value × 100, recomputed from the rounded fair value and price shown in each report so a reader can reproduce it. Negative = trading at a DISCOUNT to fair value (price below fair value); positive = at a PREMIUM. The index column shows the Lynch fair-value figure; each ticker report shows its TTM EPS, annual growth rates, capped/floored rate and fair value in full.
  • Method — Peter Lynch fair value: Fair Value = growth rate × TTM diluted EPS (GAAP unless a report notes otherwise). The growth rate is the arithmetic average of the last 5 annual year-on-year net income growth rates (6 years of net income), floored at 5% and capped at 25% AFTER averaging. If TTM EPS is negative there is no fair value — shown as N/A with the reason. The same EPS-based method is used for banks, insurers, REITs and utilities: there is no sector-specific restatement and no net-debt term in this method. Fair values are computed from unrounded growth rates and EPS, as the site does; displayed inputs are rounded, so multiplying the displayed rounded inputs may differ from the shown fair value by a few cents (largest observed in this set: $0.16, META) and discount/premium by up to 0.1pp.
  • What this method does NOT capture: it is a growth × EPS heuristic, not a cash-flow valuation. It uses no balance-sheet, moat, forward-estimate, buyback or dividend information, and it inherits every distortion in the earnings history — loss years, impairments, spin-offs and COVID-base effects are flagged in the individual reports. Where the average growth rate is meaningless (loss years, near-zero bases) the floored/capped result is mechanical and is labelled as such; do not quote it bare.
  • 5-yr CAGR and % to wtd (both tables, immediately after the 5-yr weighted column): % to wtd = (5-yr weighted − Price) / Price × 100; 5-yr CAGR = (5-yr weighted / Price)^(1/5) − 1, as a % — the compound annual growth rate implied if the price moved from the 30 Oct 2026 close to the 5-year scenario weighted price over 5 years. Both are computed from each row’s existing price and 5-yr weighted values (SNDK uses split-adjusted figures: price ~$143.86, weighted ~$127). Non-numeric weighteds (“see artifact”, “—”, qualitative-only) and unparseable row prices render as “N/A” for both columns. The 5-yr weighted is the v1 scenario weighted price, NOT the intrinsic value — it is a different figure from the Peter Lynch fair value column and must not be read as one.
  • N/A names — only three: GILD (negative TTM GAAP EPS, −$3.12, on a TTM net loss driven by ~$12.4B acquired IPR&D charges), SPCX (negative TTM EPS; listed June 2026, no 5-year listed series) and SONY (negative consolidated TTM EPS, −$0.24, including the discontinued Financial Services loss after the 1 Oct 2025 spin-off). ORCL and DE now carry normal Lynch values — their withdrawn-DCF notes are gone — and SFTBY now carries a Lynch value ($69.50); the old “no DCF” treatment no longer applies to any name.
  • Extreme values carry their own caveats in their reports — do not quote them bare: NBIS $0.80 is mechanical (TTM EPS $0.16; the series breaks at the Yandex divestiture); BE $8.60 is floored with a prominent warning (TTM EPS positive $1.72 but all five years losses); HOOD $10.85 is floored/mechanical (near-zero 2020 base); MU $1,858.25 reflects TTM EPS $74.33 on the FY2026 earnings surge, capped at 25%; MRVL $14.90 is floored (negative average growth).
  • Currency and date caveats: 2222 fair values are in SAR and its price (SAR 25.92) is a 2 Oct 2026 snapshot, NOT a verified 30 Oct close; CNOOC fair values are in CNY with the same date caveat. SNDK is split-adjusted throughout (8-for-1 split payable ~19 Nov 2026; adjusted price ~$143.86, adjusted 5-yr weighted ~$127). Thin OTC prints (PCCYF, CUAEF, GMBXF, AIQUF, ZIJMF and others flagged in v1) remain indicative only.
  • Customers: B2B tables list the top 7 customers by revenue importance where disclosed, otherwise the largest known customers with ranking explicitly not disclosed; revenue shares appear only where disclosed. A customer’s Peter Lynch fair value and discount/premium vs Lynch FV are shown only if that customer is itself one of the 124 covered tickers (taken from this same build); private or uncovered customers read “not in coverage — no intrinsic on file”; N/A customers (GILD, SPCX, SONY) read “N/A” with the reason. The read-through thesis (undervalued customers as a potential tailwind) is stated with its caveat in each report: an undervalued customer does not guarantee higher spending.
  • Unchanged by this revision: verdicts, scenario prices (including all 5-yr weighted prices) and published weights are UNCHANGED. A large premium vs Lynch fair value is a statement about what the growth × EPS heuristic implies, not a new sell call.
  • Verification: in this revision every Disc/Prem % was recomputed from the rounded Lynch fair value and 30 Oct price shown, every customer Lynch figure was recomputed from the same master data, and the 5-yr CAGR / % to wtd columns were checked against worked examples (NVDA: +21.8% CAGR, +167.8% to wtd; SNDK adjusted: −2.5% CAGR, −11.7% to wtd). Lynch values were cross-checked against valueinvesting.io published fair values where the site covers the ticker — approximately close, or the variance is explained in the report. All intrinsic figures remain MODELLED ESTIMATES from stated inputs and assumptions, not verified facts and not investment advice. Inputs that could not be re-sourced are labelled UNVERIFIED/estimate in their reports, never presented as fact.

Glossary

Every term used in the tables above and in the linked reports.

ADR
American Depositary Receipt — a US-listed certificate representing shares of a foreign company (e.g. TSM, TM), so the price is in USD but the underlying trades abroad.
AFFO
Adjusted Funds From Operations — FFO further adjusted for recurring capital spending and straight-line rent; the closer REIT analogue of free cash flow.
Bear / Base / Bull case
The pessimistic / central / optimistic scenario in a valuation. Each carries explicit assumptions and a probability (scenario weight); none is a promise.
Buyback
A company repurchasing its own shares, which reduces the share count and lifts per-share figures; part of capital allocation alongside dividends and reinvestment.
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate — the smoothed yearly growth rate between two values over a period.
Consensus
The average of sell-side analyst estimates or price targets. “Consensus only” means the source report carries the consensus anchor and no modelled Bull/Bear split.
D/E (debt-to-equity)
Total debt divided by shareholders' equity — a leverage gauge. Higher means more of the business is funded by borrowing.
Data-centre pipeline
The contracted and planned capacity (usually in MW/GW) a utility, REIT or developer has agreed or announced to build for data-centre customers — a forward growth indicator.
Dividend yield
Annual dividend per share divided by the share price — the income return before any price movement.
EPS
Earnings per share — net income divided by the share count. GAAP EPS includes one-offs; non-GAAP/underlying EPS strips them out.
EV/EBITDA
Enterprise value (market cap plus net debt) divided by earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a capital-structure-neutral valuation multiple.
FCF (free cash flow)
Operating cash flow minus capital expenditure — the cash left for dividends, buybacks, debt repayment or reinvestment.
FFO
Funds From Operations — the REIT earnings measure: net income plus depreciation/amortisation of real estate, excluding property sale gains.
Frozen-parameter model
The 5-year projection method used in this series: today's net margin, P/E and share count are held (“frozen”) unless a case changes them, and only revenue growth varies by scenario.
LOE (loss of exclusivity)
The date a drug's patent protection ends and generic/biosimilar competition can enter — a key pharma downside risk (relevant to MRK, PFE, ABBV).
MFN (most favoured nation)
In this series, US drug-pricing policy pressure to match the lowest price paid in comparable countries — a margin risk for large pharma names.
Merger arbitrage
Trading a stock on the spread between its market price and an agreed takeover price; the return depends on the deal closing, not on standalone fundamentals (see D).
NAV discount
When a holding company or conglomerate trades below the estimated net asset value of what it owns — the gap is the discount (relevant to SFTBY, BRK.B, TCEHY).
Net margin
Net income divided by revenue — how much of each sales dollar survives as profit.
OTC
Over-the-counter — traded off a major exchange, often thinly, so prices are indicative and sometimes unverified (many foreign names here are OTC ADRs).
P/AFFO
Price divided by AFFO per share — the REIT multiple that pairs with FFO-based valuation instead of P/E.
P/E — trailing
Price divided by the last twelve months' actual EPS. Meaningless or misleading when earnings are depressed or one-off-heavy.
P/E — forward
Price divided by expected EPS over the next twelve months — depends on analyst estimates being right.
P/FFO
Price divided by FFO per share — the standard REIT valuation multiple, used in this series instead of trailing P/E for real estate.
P/S (price-to-sales)
Market cap divided by revenue — useful when earnings are thin or negative, but it ignores profitability entirely.
Rate base
The asset base on which a regulated utility is allowed to earn a regulated return; growing the rate base (e.g. grid and data-centre build-out) grows allowed earnings.
REIT
Real Estate Investment Trust — a property company that must pay out most taxable income as dividends and is valued on FFO/AFFO rather than EPS.
ROE (return on equity)
Net income divided by shareholders' equity — how profitably a company uses its owners' capital.
Scenario weights
The probabilities attached to Bear / Base / Bull cases (commonly 25% / 50% / 25% in this series) used to blend them into a weighted price.
SHOP (senior housing operating portfolio)
The segment of a healthcare REIT (e.g. WELL, VTR) where the REIT shares in the operating economics of senior housing, rather than only collecting rent.
Triple-net (NNN)
A lease where the tenant pays rent plus taxes, insurance and maintenance — the landlord's income is comparatively passive and predictable (e.g. O).
Verdict factor
The normalisation rule in this index: Buy-family = 1.0, Hold-family = 0.5, Sell = 0, applied to a published weight to get its effective weight for a horizon.
Weighted price
The probability-weighted blend of the Bear, Base and Bull scenario prices — read as “the maths under these assumptions”, not a prediction.
