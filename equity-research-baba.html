01

Business model & segment breakdown

Alibaba is a holding-company ecosystem built around commerce, with cloud and logistics monetising the same merchant and consumer base. It is China-heavy, expanding internationally — a geographic concentration that explains both the cash generation and the persistent valuation discount.

🛒 Core commerce — Taobao & Tmall

Marketplace commissions, advertising / customer-management revenue, and direct sales. The profit engine that funds everything else. Efficiency — not just GMV growth — is the current focus.

☁️ Alibaba Cloud

Cloud infrastructure and AI services. Identified in the pack as the growth engine, growing double-digits (rate not re-verified — treat as directional, not a reported %). This is the pivot investors are paying to see scale.

🌏 International commerce

AliExpress, Lazada, and Trendyol. Competes directly with Amazon and Temu outside China; higher growth potential, lower certainty than domestic commerce.

🚚 Cainiao — logistics

Warehousing and delivery network supporting commerce at home and cross-border. A dependency and a moat: fulfilment quality underpins conversion.

📍 Local services

Local consumer services tied to the app ecosystem. Strategically useful for frequency, not yet the valuation driver.

💳 Ecosystem dependencies

Payments adjacency, logistics, and cloud are interdependent. Disruption in any one — regulation, merchant churn, or cloud competition — transmits to the others.

Structural pivot (per pack): AI / cloud as the growth engine, e-commerce run for efficiency, and shareholder returns (buybacks + dividend) now material. That is a shift from “grow every segment” to “fund cloud from commerce cash, return the rest.” Verify the buyback pace each quarter — it is part of the thesis, not a side note.

03

Competitive moat & industry positioning

What protects it

  • Marketplace scale: Taobao/Tmall network effects — merchants go where buyers are.
  • Logistics + payments adjacency: Cainiao fulfilment and the broader ecosystem raise switching costs.
  • Cloud scale in China: One of the few domestic providers able to fund AI infrastructure at scale.

Where it is contested

  • JD — direct retail / logistics rival.
  • Pinduoduo / Temu (PDD) — price-led competition at home and abroad; the pack's named pressure.
  • Tencent & ByteDance / Douyin commerce — attention and social commerce.
  • Amazon — internationally.

Pricing power is segment-dependent: strong where Alibaba controls discovery and fulfilment, weak where PDD competes purely on price and Douyin controls attention. The moat is real but not unassailable — and it does not protect against the non-competitive risks in §4 (regulation, ADR status), which is why position size stays at 2% despite the scale.

04

Catalysts & downside risks

▲ Catalysts

  • Cloud / AI acceleration — re-rating if double-digit growth sustains and monetises
  • International commerce scaling (AliExpress / Trendyol)
  • Buybacks + dividend — visible cash return supporting the share count maths in §5
  • E-commerce efficiency gains lifting margins without heroic growth

▼ Risks

  • China regulation — platform, data, and antitrust action can reprice the stock overnight
  • Consumer weakness in China — hits core commerce first
  • US–China / ADR risk — listing, audit, and geopolitical overhang specific to the ADR
  • Competition from PDD and Douyin commerce compressing take rates

Risk note for sizing: these risks are correlated. A regulatory or geopolitical shock would likely hit price, multiple, and sentiment together — diversification inside China equities does not diversify this exposure.

PV of projected FCF $98.78bn + PV of terminal value $95.46bn + net cash $17.89bn, divided by 2.488bn diluted shares = $85.26. Terminal value = Year-10 FCF × 1.03 / (r − 0.03), discounted at r.

Read-through. Alibaba discloses no single merchant or Cloud customer above 10% of revenue, so the read-through is aggregate: if its merchant and Cloud-customer base were broadly trading at a discount to intrinsic, that would be a potential tailwind for commissions, advertising and Cloud spend, and broad cash pressure would be the reverse. But an undervalued customer does not guarantee higher spending, which depends on each merchant's own priorities and cash position, and for Alibaba the larger swing factor is Chinese consumer demand behind those merchants, not any one merchant's valuation.

AI trade positioning — BABA

Role: Beneficiary. Alibaba is an AI beneficiary through Cloud Intelligence (Qwen models) and AI-driven e-commerce efficiency: the pack's pivot is AI/cloud as the growth engine, funded — painfully — by the core marketplace, with TTM FCF turned negative on AI and quick-commerce spend.

While the AI trade runs. While the AI trade runs, demand grows through Cloud AI workloads and better marketplace conversion; the multiple is supported by the market treating Alibaba as a cheaper AI/cloud re-rating story (forward P/E ~12x in the pack's data).

When the AI trade unwinds. If the trade unwinds, demand breaks first in Cloud AI orders and in quick-commerce spending that no longer pays; then the multiple compresses toward a slower-growth China e-commerce multiple; then the balance sheet — cash $57.9bn against debt $40.0bn — is a cushion, but the cash-burn pace is the constraint. Watch: Cloud growth, FCF returning to positive, and any China regulatory or VIE pressure.
