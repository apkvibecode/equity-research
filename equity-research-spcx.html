3   Competitive moat & positioning

  • Launch cost & cadence — strongest moat: 165 Falcon launches in 2025, reuse record 37 flights on one booster, vertical integration and internal Starlink demand create a cost loop rivals cannot match without equal cadence. Falcon 9 listed ~$74m/launch; internal cost modelled <$30m. Market-share figures for cargo mass to orbit in the pack were UNVERIFIED in verification — treat share claims as estimates.
  • Constellation scale: ~9,600+ satellites vs Amazon Leo/Kuiper at deployment stage, Eutelsat/OneWeb far smaller. First-mover spectrum, ground stations and terminal manufacturing help, but are not permanent.
  • Government entrenchment: NASA crew/cargo, Space Force/NRO, $63bn backlog. High switching costs — and high political exposure.
  • AI compute — UNPROVEN moat: Nameplate compute and “<13 GW additions” guidance from the call were not fully verified in context — UNVERIFIED. If SI contracts are long-dated and high-margin, this becomes a second moat; if they are short-cancellable rentals, it is commodity rental at mega-cap prices.

Pricing power is mixed: Launch — price-setter. Starlink — ARPU $99 → $69 shows discounting to sustain volume. SI — pricing power UNVERIFIED (pricing per GW, renewal rates not disclosed).

Competitors: Launch — Blue Origin, Rocket Lab (RKLB), ULA, Arianespace, Chinese programmes. Broadband — Amazon Leo, Eutelsat/OneWeb, AST SpaceMobile, GEO incumbents. AI compute — hyperscalers (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN) and neoclouds, who have equal/greater capital and enterprise relationships. Social/AI models — X/Grok vs OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Google.

Barriers: capital ($20bn+/yr capex), licences, physics of reuse, talent, launch-site scarcity. Those protect launch today; they do not protect SI.
