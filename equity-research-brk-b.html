Read the P/E with care. Berkshire's trailing P/E of 13.67× embeds unrealised gains and losses on its huge equity portfolio, which flow through reported earnings under mark-to-market accounting. That is why the forward P/E (23.90×) sits above the trailing one — analysts expect lower reported earnings ahead, not because the stock suddenly got expensive. For Berkshire, price-to-book (~1.6×) and operating-earnings power are the more honest gauges than either P/E.

1 · Business model & segment breakdown

Berkshire is not one business — it is a holding company that collects cash from mature operating companies and redeploys it. Its core engines:

  • Insurance — GEICO (US auto insurance) and a large reinsurance operation. Insurance is the heart of the model: premiums are collected up front and paid out later, creating float — other people's money that Berkshire invests in the meantime at effectively negative cost when underwriting is disciplined.
  • BNSF Railway — one of North America's largest freight railroads; steady, regulated, capital-intensive cash generation.
  • Berkshire Hathaway Energy — regulated utilities and energy infrastructure, another long-duration, rate-regulated cash stream.
  • Manufacturing, service & retail — a wide bench of wholly owned operating companies across industrial products, consumer goods, services and retail.
  • The listed-equity portfolio and cash pile — a huge portfolio of publicly traded stocks plus substantial cash, funded by float and retained operating earnings.

No segment profit split was verified in this research pass, so this page deliberately does not quote a single segment profit figure rather than present an unchecked one. Total TTM revenue of $438.3B (Motley Fool) is the verified top line.

The structural pivot: succession

The defining change is at the top. Warren Buffett retired as CEO at the end of 2025 and Greg Abel took over; Buffett then stepped down as chairman in September 2026 (per Motley Fool). Berkshire is therefore, for the first time in six decades, a company whose investment case rests on a system and a successor rather than on Buffett personally. Funding and regulation follow the model: Berkshire is financed by insurance float and operating cash flow — not bank deposits — and its insurance, utility and rail units are each regulated in their own industries.

Balance-sheet character: Berkshire's moat-grade balance sheet is the point of the company — a fortress of cash and liquid securities funded substantially by insurance float rather than debt-funded leverage. Capital allocation is the product: operating cash flows in, and Abel's team decides between acquisitions, listed equities, buybacks, or simply holding cash until prices cooperate. The open question of this era is not solvency — it is whether the enormous cash position gets deployed at attractive returns or sits as a drag.

3 · Competitive moat & industry positioning

What protects it

  • Float-funded investing: insurance float gives Berkshire permanent, low-cost capital that no ordinary asset manager can replicate.
  • Scale: few buyers on earth can write a multi-billion-dollar cheque without financing contingencies — Berkshire can, which wins deals.
  • Diversified cash flows: insurance, rail, energy and manufacturing rarely slump in the same way at the same time.
  • Capital-allocation reputation: built under Buffett, now Abel's to keep — sellers of good businesses have historically preferred Berkshire as a permanent home.
  • Fortress balance sheet: the cash pile is both a shield in crises and an option on other people's distress.

Who it competes with

Berkshire competes business-by-business rather than as a whole: other large insurers and reinsurers in insurance, the other Class I railroads against BNSF, regulated utility peers in energy, and every large asset manager and conglomerate for acquisitions and listed equities. The honest assessment from the research: no competitor replicates the float + portfolio + operating-company combination at this scale — the moat is the structure itself, not any single product.

4 · Catalysts & downside risks

Catalysts

  • Cash deployment — a major acquisition or a large equity buying programme would convert the cash drag into earnings power.
  • Insurance pricing cycle — firm pricing at GEICO and in reinsurance flows almost directly to underwriting profit and grows float.
  • BNSF / energy earnings recovery — improvement in the rail and utility engines lifts operating earnings, the number that matters most here.

Risks

  • Succession execution under Abel — the first real test of the post-Buffett system; any capital-allocation stumble reprices the reputation premium.
  • Equity-portfolio concentration — the listed portfolio has historically been concentrated (notably in Apple), so a single name can move reported results.
  • Catastrophe losses — a severe hurricane or disaster season hits insurance earnings directly.
  • Utility regulation & liability exposure — regulated returns can be squeezed, and wildfire-type liabilities are a live risk for US utilities generally.
  • Reported-earnings volatility — mark-to-market swings will keep printing alarming or flattering headline EPS numbers that say little about the operating business.

6 · Recommendation & position sizing

Short term — Hold. At ~1.6× book with a new CEO, a large undeployed cash position and no near-term catalyst beyond insurance pricing, there is no rush; the 12–18-month base case offers only ~+7%.

Long term — Buy. The structure — float, diversification, fortress balance sheet — survives the founder. For a mid-30s investor who can hold through the succession proof-period, Berkshire is a core-quality compounder bought for resilience rather than excitement.

Suggested weight: 4% of the total portfolio for the mid-30s, risk-8/10 profile — meaningful ballast inside the Financials sleeve, sized so its low growth doesn't dilute the portfolio's higher-octane names.

AI trade positioning — Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B)

Role: Low exposure (indirect beneficiary). While the AI trade runs, Berkshire benefits indirectly: its listed-equity portfolio and its operating businesses (rail freight, energy, manufacturing) gain from AI-driven corporate spending, and its insurers and industrials can use AI for underwriting, pricing and productivity — but the pack gives no AI revenue line for Berkshire itself, so this is second-order.

While the AI trade runs — multiple channel: The multiple channel is muted: Berkshire trades on book value (~1.6x, pack) and operating-earnings power, not an AI multiple, so an AI de-rating does not mechanically compress a Berkshire AI premium that does not exist.

When the AI trade unwinds: If the AI trade unwinds, what breaks in order is: (1) demand — portfolio holdings and BNSF freight tied to tech capex soften; (2) multiple — the portfolio’s market value falls with the market, hitting reported earnings through mark-to-market volatility (a pack-flagged risk); (3) balance sheet — low direct risk, given the fortress balance sheet and insurance float, though a catastrophe year coinciding with a market fall would compound the hit. Triggers to watch: a sharp fall in its largest listed holdings, a BNSF volume downturn, and any large deployment of cash at peak prices.
