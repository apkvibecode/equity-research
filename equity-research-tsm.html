Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Q3 2026 actuals

Pure-play foundry — it manufactures other people’s chips

TSMC does not design chips under its own brand. It fabricates designs for fabless customers and integrated customers who outsource leading-edge production. That neutrality is the model: every major AI accelerator, smartphone SoC, and hyperscaler custom-silicon programme that needs the most advanced process competes for the same TSMC capacity — so TSMC monetises the whole AI buildout without betting on a single chip winner.

Q3 2026 headline (confirmed in verification log): revenue NT$1,494.24B = $46.72B, up +50.9% in TWD / +41.2% in USD year-on-year, beating the $44.6–45.8B guide. Net income NT$763.06B, +68.7%. Gross margin 66.4%, operating margin 58.7%.

Customers & concentration

Named customers in the pack: Apple, Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm, Broadcom, plus hyperscaler custom silicon. Concentration is described in the pack as “a feature and a risk” — the same handful of customers drive the leading-edge order book, so a single customer’s inventory correction or in-sourcing move would be felt immediately, while their collective AI spending is what keeps utilisation and pricing at record levels.

Geography & structural pivot

Production remains centred on Taiwan fabs, with expansion underway in Arizona (US), Japan, and Germany. The pack explicitly flags margin dilution from the overseas build-out: overseas fabs run at higher cost than Taiwan fabs, so geographic diversification buys supply-chain resilience at the price of some margin. Supply-chain dependencies: ASML EUV lithography is the critical dependency, and advanced packaging (CoWoS) is the current constraint on AI-chip throughput.

03

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

The pack’s moat verdict: process lead (2nm ramp), scale, yield, and ecosystem — “effectively unreplicable at leading edge in <5 years.” That is the core of the investment case: even a well-funded rival cannot buy its way to TSMC’s yields, supplier ecosystem, and customer trust inside one product cycle.

Pricing power

66.4% gross margin in a manufacturing business is the evidence. When capacity is the industry bottleneck and CoWoS packaging is the constraint, the foundry — not the customer — sets the terms. Q4 guide holds gross margin at 65–67% even as 2nm ramps and overseas fabs dilute.

Barriers to entry

Three stacked barriers: (1) process technology and yield learning accumulated over decades; (2) capital — a $60–64B annual capex guide; (3) the ASML EUV supply chain and the customer ecosystem (design tools, IP libraries, packaging) built around TSMC’s nodes.

Competitors

Samsung Foundry — the only other leading-edge alternative, still chasing yield and share. Intel Foundry — rebuilding, not yet a proven external leading-edge supplier. SMIC — confined to lagging nodes under export controls. None, per the pack, replicates TSMC at the leading edge in under five years.

04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts Upside

  • AI / HPC demand exceeds supply — the pack cites the chairman’s statement that demand is ahead of supply; HPC is already 68% of revenue and Q4 is guided up to $52.3–54.3B (≈ +12–16% sequentially from Q3).
  • 2nm ramp — 2nm is already 8% of wafer revenue in Q3 2026; each point of mix shift toward 2nm supports both revenue and the technology-lead narrative.
  • CoWoS capacity — advanced packaging is the stated constraint; relieving it converts directly into shippable AI accelerators for Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom and hyperscaler silicon.
  • Geographic expansion — Arizona / Japan / Germany capacity de-risks the single-region story over time and aligns TSMC with customer and government localisation demands.

Downside risks Watch

  • Taiwan geopolitics — the single largest risk and the reason sizing is capped at 3.5% despite a double Buy. It is a low-probability, portfolio-level event that no company fundamental can offset.
  • US / China export controls — tightening rules can remove demand, complicate customer orders, and raise compliance cost.
  • Capex cycle — $60–64B of 2026 capex must be absorbed even if AI demand pauses; overseas fabs dilute margin while they ramp.
  • Customer inventory correction — with HPC at 68% of sales and a concentrated customer set, an AI-accelerator inventory digestion would hit utilisation fast.
  • Single-supplier dependency — ASML EUV is the critical dependency; any disruption there caps TSMC’s expansion regardless of demand.

TSM — AI trade positioning

Role: Enabler — the foundry bottleneck. TSMC makes the chips the AI trade runs on: Q3 2026 revenue NT$1,494.24B ($46.72B, +41.2% USD YoY), HPC 68% of revenue, 7nm-and-below 79% of wafer revenue, and the pack cites the chairman's statement that AI/HPC demand exceeds supply.

While the AI trade runs, demand comes through leading-edge wafer orders and CoWoS advanced packaging, which the pack calls the constraint, with Q4 guided to $52.3–54.3B. On multiples, TSMC's ADR trades around 24.6–26.3x trailing earnings in the pack — rich for a manufacturer, but supported while demand exceeds supply and gross margin prints 66.4%.

If the AI trade unwinds, demand breaks first through customer inventory correction and order cuts, hitting the most advanced nodes last but hitting CoWoS and mature-node utilisation first; the pack flags margin dilution from Arizona/Japan/Germany expansion as a second-order drag. Multiple compression follows toward a through-cycle foundry multiple, well below the current premium. The balance sheet is the mitigant: roughly $79B of net cash on the figures used here, against a $60–64B 2026 capex guide that can be flexed. Triggers to watch: Q4 delivery against guide, 2nm ramp yields, CoWoS capacity commentary, and any Taiwan-geopolitics or export-control escalation.
