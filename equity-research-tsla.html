01Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Tesla today is three businesses wearing one ticker, plus an option on a fourth:

Vehicles — the revenue engine

Model 3/Y are the volume core, with Cybertruck and Semi extending the range. Manufacturing spans the US, China and Europe, which diversifies plant risk but concentrates exposure to US–China trade and European demand at the same time. Recent price cuts have defended volume at the cost of auto gross margin — the pack flags that compression explicitly, and it is the single most important operating fact in the near-term story.

Energy, services & the option

Energy generation & storage is growing and integrates with the vehicle and charging ecosystem. Services (including the charging network) monetise the installed fleet. Layered on top is the autonomy / robotics optionality — FSD, Robotaxi and Optimus — which is not yet the revenue base but is, per the research pack, what the investment case now rests on.

The structural pivot

The pivot is from car-maker to AI / robotics company. That sentence does real work in the valuation: a car-maker is priced on units and auto margins; an autonomy platform is priced on fleet miles, take-rate and software margins. Tesla's current price only makes sense in the second frame — §5 quantifies how much of today's $400 that frame has to deliver.

Supply-chain dependencies

  • Batteries: lithium and nickel exposure, plus Tesla's own 4680 cell programme — cost and energy-density progress here flows straight into vehicle margin.
  • Semiconductors: shared with the entire auto and AI complex; no Tesla-specific verified figure is carried in the pack, so none is asserted here.
  • China supply base: both a cost advantage and a geopolitical single point of failure — see §4.

Open question, stated plainly: the research pack does not carry a verified segment-by-segment revenue split or geographic revenue split for Tesla. Rather than fill that gap with unverified figures, this report works from the verified totals and labels the one revenue figure it uses — ~$100bn — as an estimate wherever it appears.

03Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Where the moat is real

  • Brand — still the default name in EVs for most buyers.
  • Charging network (NACS) — the standard others have adopted; a genuine infrastructure advantage.
  • Manufacturing scale — cost position built over a decade of gigafactory learning.
  • Software & data for FSD — fleet data is the raw material autonomy is trained on.
  • Energy integration — car, storage, charging and software under one roof.

Where it is not

  • Pricing power in EVs is weak — the pack says this directly, and repeated price cuts prove it. A moat that requires cutting price to hold volume is a scale moat, not a pricing moat.
  • Autonomy leadership is contested — Waymo competes from the other direction (geofenced, sensor-heavy), and regulators, not just engineers, set the pace.

Competitors named in the pack: BYD and Chinese EV makers on cost and volume, legacy OEMs on distribution and balance sheets, Rivian in the US EV niche, and Waymo in autonomy. The honest summary: Tesla leads on integration and brand, is challenged on price in China, and has not yet converted its autonomy lead into verified, scaled robotaxi economics — which is exactly the gap the share price assumes away.

04Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts

  • Robotaxi scaling — the first hard evidence that autonomy revenue is real and repeatable
  • FSD licensing to other OEMs — high-margin revenue with no extra factories
  • Optimus progress from demo to deployable product
  • Energy margins as storage scale compounds
  • Lower-cost models reopening the mass-market volume story

Risks

  • Auto demand and an EV price war compressing margins further
  • Autonomy regulation and safety events — a single serious incident can reset timelines
  • China competition and geopolitical exposure on both demand and supply
  • Key-person concentration — the story, the compensation and the valuation are unusually tied to one individual
  • Valuation itself: at $400 the price requires autonomy success; disappointment has no cushion

06Verdict & Portfolio Sizing

Short term — HOLD

Flat base case ($400), wide cone ($280–$520). There is no edge in chasing a stock whose 12–18-month expected value is its current price and whose downside case is −30%.

Long term — HOLD, do not add at $400

The bull case is real — autonomy at scale would justify well above today's price — but at $400 you are paying for it in advance with no margin of safety. The pack's instruction is explicit: hold an existing position, do not add here. Revisit on either a materially lower price or verified robotaxi unit economics.

Position sizer — 3% maximum, satellite only

For a mid-30s investor at risk 8/10, Tesla belongs in the high-risk satellite sleeve, capped at 3% of the total portfolio — sized so the bear case (−92% on the name) costs the portfolio under 3 points, while the bull case still matters.

Intrinsic value — TSLA (30 Oct 2026 close)

Price (30 Oct 2026 close): $399.81. Peter Lynch fair value: $22.75. Discount/premium: +1,657.4% — i.e. the shares are trading 1,657.4% above the Lynch fair value. Discount/premium % = (Price − Fair value) / Fair value × 100; negative = discount, positive = premium. Upside to fair value (secondary figure): -94.3% = (Fair value − Price) / Price × 100.

Method — Peter Lynch fair value = Earnings Growth Rate × TTM diluted EPS. The growth rate is the arithmetic average of the 5 annual year-over-year net-income growth rates (6 years of annual net income attributable to common stockholders, calendar years 2020–2025), floored at 5% and capped at 25% after averaging.

AI trade positioning — TSLA

Role: Beneficiary / At-risk. Tesla's investment case now rests on AI/robotics optionality (FSD, Robotaxi, Optimus) rather than vehicle volume, per the pack — but that optionality is not yet paid revenue, and TTM net income is only $3.22bn on $103.6bn of revenue.

While the AI trade runs. While the AI trade runs, demand grows through autonomy licensing, Robotaxi deployment and energy/AI compute narratives; the multiple is supported by the market capitalising autonomy at a premium (the pack's bull case needs a 45x multiple on 18% margins).

When the AI trade unwinds. If the trade unwinds, demand breaks first in autonomy timelines and Robotaxi milestones slipping, then in vehicle pricing as the AI premium is stripped out; then the multiple compresses violently from ~440x trailing earnings toward an auto multiple (the pack itself calls the trailing P/E meaningless and shows a $31 bear case on mature margins); then the balance sheet — net cash $29.0bn — is a cushion, not a defence of the multiple. Watch: FSD/Robotaxi regulatory milestones, vehicle deliveries and price cuts, and capex above the $25bn guide.
