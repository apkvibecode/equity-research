01

Business model & segment breakdown

Broadcom runs two businesses that look unrelated until you see the cash flows. The semiconductor side designs custom AI accelerators (ASICs) built to individual hyperscalers' specifications, plus the networking silicon (Tomahawk and Jericho switching/router families), wireless and storage components that move data around and into those accelerators. The infrastructure software side is anchored by VMware — virtualisation and private-cloud software sold on subscription to large enterprises.

The structural pivot is the speed of the AI line. In fiscal Q3 2026, AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7B of $29.59B total revenue — 56% of the quarter, up 221% year over year and 54% sequentially. The fiscal Q4 guide points the same way: ~$34.8B total revenue with AI semiconductors at ~$21.7B (62% of the guided total). A company that was a diversified chip-and-software compounder two years ago is now, on the margin, an AI-infrastructure supplier with a very profitable software annuity attached.

Revenue streams

  • Custom AI accelerators / ASICs — designed with, and sold to, a small number of hyperscalers; the $16.7B FQ3 line, guided to $21.7B in FQ4.
  • Networking silicon — Tomahawk/Jericho Ethernet switching for AI data-centre fabrics.
  • Other semiconductors — wireless, storage and broadband connectivity components.
  • Infrastructure software — VMware-led subscription software; slower-growing, high-margin, and the ballast when chip cycles turn.

Dependencies to know

  • Customer concentration: the custom-ASIC business rests on 2–3 hyperscaler customers. Losing one design slot — or one customer shifting in-house — moves the whole growth story.
  • Hyperscaler capex: AVGO's AI revenue is a direct derivative of a handful of customers' data-centre budgets and their willingness to keep spending before AI returns are proven.
  • Advanced-node supply: custom accelerators depend on leading-edge foundry capacity and advanced packaging shared with every other AI chipmaker.

03

Competitive moat & industry positioning

Where the moat is real

  • Custom-ASIC design wins: a hyperscaler that co-designs an accelerator with Broadcom is locked in for that chip's multi-year life — re-designing with a rival means re-doing the silicon and the software stack around it.
  • Networking depth: Tomahawk/Jericho Ethernet silicon is embedded in AI data-centre fabrics; in AI clusters, the network is the computer, and Broadcom is a default supplier.
  • VMware lock-in: enterprise virtualisation estates are painful and risky to migrate, giving the software arm durable, recurring, high-margin revenue.
  • Scale M&A playbook: a proven ability to acquire, integrate and extract cash from large franchises — itself a barrier smaller rivals can't copy.

Who it fights

  • Nvidia — the merchant-GPU default; every custom-ASIC win is silicon a hyperscaler chose not to buy from Nvidia, and Nvidia defends that turf aggressively.
  • Marvell — the closest custom-silicon and interconnect rival, competing for the same hyperscaler design slots.
  • AMD — merchant accelerators and CPUs competing for the same AI budgets.
  • Cisco — networking incumbent contesting the Ethernet-for-AI fabric.
  • Customers themselves — hyperscalers' in-house silicon teams are both the buyers and a standing alternative.

Net assessment: the moat is strong but rented. Design wins and VMware lock-in are genuine, multi-year advantages — but the largest ones renew at the pleasure of two or three customers whose capex decisions Broadcom does not control. Pricing power today is excellent (75.5% gross margin is the evidence); its durability is the open question.

04

Catalysts & downside risks

Catalysts

  • FQ4 delivery: hitting the ~$34.8B total / $21.7B AI guide would confirm the +221% AI trajectory is still steepening, not plateauing.
  • Next-generation custom accelerators — successive design generations with existing hyperscaler customers deepen the lock-in and the revenue per customer.
  • Ethernet for AI: as AI clusters scale out, Ethernet fabric attach (Tomahawk/Jericho) grows with every accelerator shipped — including competitors'.
  • VMware subscription conversion — continued migration of the installed base to subscriptions supports the software margin engine.

Downside risks

  • Customer concentration: 2–3 hyperscalers drive the AI line. A single lost design slot or capex pause is a double-digit revenue event, not a rounding error.
  • AI-capex payback: if hyperscalers' AI returns disappoint, custom-silicon orders are among the first lines cut — AVGO's growth multiple would compress with them.
  • VMware integration & regulation: licensing changes have drawn customer and regulatory scrutiny; remedies or churn would hit the highest-margin segment.
  • Debt-funded M&A: $59.42B of debt (definition note in §2) leaves less room for error if the next deal disappoints or rates stay high.
  • Valuation itself: at 40×+ trailing earnings and ~17.5× sales, the price already assumes years of flawless AI growth — small misses get punished disproportionately.

AVGO — AI trade positioning

Role: Enabler — custom silicon and AI networking. Broadcom is the merchant alternative to Nvidia inside the AI build-out: fiscal Q3 2026 AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B (+221% YoY) and a fiscal Q4 guide of ~$34.8B total with AI semis at $21.7B, per the pack.

While the AI trade runs, demand comes through hyperscaler custom-accelerator generations and Ethernet for AI clusters (Tomahawk/Jericho), plus VMware software as the recurring base. On multiples, Broadcom trades at 40–41x trailing GAAP earnings in the pack (forward 16.8–19.4x on a non-GAAP basis the pack warns to use with caution), so the AI narrative is already capitalised in a premium that depends on the custom-silicon ramp sustaining.

If the AI trade unwinds, demand breaks first through hyperscaler custom-program delays or cancellations and a shift back toward merchant GPUs, hitting the AI semiconductor leg before VMware. Multiple compression follows from the GAAP/non-GAAP gap closing the wrong way. Balance-sheet risk is real but manageable: $59.42B of debt against $23.98B of cash in the pack, a legacy of debt-funded M&A, and customer concentration in two to three hyperscalers is the concentration risk. Triggers to watch: the next custom-accelerator design-win announcements, AI semiconductor revenue versus the $21.7B guide, and any hyperscaler in-sourcing announcement.
