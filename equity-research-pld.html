Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Prologis is the global landlord to the goods economy: warehouses, distribution centres and flex logistics space sited in consumption markets — close to end consumers, ports and population density — leased to 6,500+ customers on a platform with $240B of assets under management. The model is rent plus embedded growth: long leases signed in a weaker rent era are now rolling to market, development and value-add activity captures the spread, and the land bank in supply-constrained infill markets is the scarce input competitors cannot replicate quickly.

Structural pivots (2025–26)

  • From pure logistics to logistics + powered land: the 5.8 GW data-centre pipeline reframes part of the land bank as AI infrastructure. Same scarce inputs (infill land, power interconnection), higher rent per acre if executed — and materially higher capex intensity if Prologis builds rather than sells powered land.
  • Transatlantic scale via SEGRO: a ~$18.8B combination, approved by SEGRO holders 28 Sep 2026 and expected to close H1 2027, deepens the European footprint to a scale no listed rival matches on both sides of the Atlantic.
  • Harvesting the COVID-era rent reset: the investment case has rotated from development gains (the zero-rate era) to internal growth — mark-to-market burn-off flowing through same-store NOI at 7%+ guided rates, which needs no new construction to deliver.

Supply-chain & operating dependencies

  • Goods flows are the demand function: tenant demand tracks consumption, imports and e-commerce fulfilment. Tariffs or a trade shock that reduces goods movement hits leasing velocity first, rents second — see §4.
  • Power: the data-centre thesis depends on grid interconnection and secured power (5.8 GW secured/advanced) actually converting to delivered capacity on schedule.
  • Construction & capital costs: development economics are rate- and cost-sensitive; a 10-year above 5% raises both the discount rate on the stock and the hurdle rate on new builds simultaneously.
  • Capital markets access: as a REIT, Prologis funds growth externally by design — equity and debt market windows are an operating dependency, not just a valuation one.

Read this REIT on FFO, not earnings. The cash economics improved; the accounting line fell. 03

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Moat rating: wide — location scarcity at global scale. Warehouses are commodity boxes; land next to consumption is not. Prologis assembled infill positions in the world's major consumption markets over decades, at historical land costs. A competitor can build a shed; it cannot rebuild the land bank at anything like the embedded cost — and the ~16% mark-to-market is the measurable residue of that advantage.

Moat strengths

  • Global scale, local scarcity: $240B AUM, 6,500+ customers, and — post-SEGRO — a European portfolio to 363m sq ft alongside the US platform. Tenants running global supply chains get one landlord across continents.
  • Embedded pricing power: ~16% mark-to-market (~$780m NOI, per call summary) means rents can grow on rollover alone even if market rents go flat — pricing power already signed, waiting to vest.
  • Data as an operating edge: leasing and throughput data across thousands of tenants and markets informs siting, development and retention decisions at a granularity no single-market rival sees.
  • Power + land optionality: 5.8 GW of secured/advanced data-centre power converts the same land moat into the AI-infrastructure market — an adjacency private logistics rivals cannot fund as cheaply.
  • Customer diversification: 6,500+ customers caps single-tenant risk; the Q3 print (60m sq ft leased, 95.6% occupancy in a supply wave) is the moat showing up in numbers.

Moat limits & competitors

  • GLP (private) — the closest global-scale logistics rival, private and aggressive in Asia and data centres; competes for the same tenants and land.
  • Digital Realty (DLR) — not a logistics rival, but the direct competitor for the data-centre leg and for the marginal REIT investor dollar in powered real estate.
  • STAG Industrial & Rexford (REXR) — US-focused industrial REITs competing on secondary-market and coastal-infill niches respectively; smaller, sometimes cheaper per unit of FFO growth.
  • No moat against the rate: the moat protects NOI, not the multiple. At a 10-year above 5%, PLD de-rated on a beat — duration risk is the honest limit of the moat.
  • New supply: the 2022–24 development wave delivered competing space; occupancy held, but market-rent growth has normalised from its peak — the mark-to-market is finite and burns off.

Positioning verdict: #2 by market cap in the VNQ real-estate universe and the clear global #1 in logistics specifically. Pricing power: demonstrated in the NOI guide raise. Barriers to entry: land, entitlements and power in infill markets — time and capital, in that order. Regulatory angle: the SEGRO combination (approved by SEGRO holders 28 Sep 2026, close expected H1 2027) still carries execution and integration risk into 2027 — see §4.

04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts (12–18 months)

  • Mark-to-market burn-off (the base engine) — ~16% embedded uplift converting at lease expiry; FY26 cash NOI already guided to 7.0–7.5%. Each quarter of delivery de-risks the §5 base case without any heroic assumption.
  • SEGRO close (H1 2027) — completion of the ~$18.8B combination converts an approved deal into consolidated European scale (363m sq ft) and potential synergy disclosure.
  • Data-centre conversion — any signed build-to-suit, powered-land sale or JV from the 5.8 GW pipeline gives the market a priced data point for a leg currently valued on narrative.
  • Rate relief — the mirror of the Q3 selloff: the stock fell 3.8% on a beat because the 10-year crossed 5%. A retrace in long rates mechanically re-rates a 18.8× P/FFO long-duration cash flow.
  • FY27 guidance (with Q4 print) — a 2027 core FFO guide implying mid-single-digit growth or better, with NOI still guided high-single-digit, would confirm the burn-off runway extends beyond 2026.

Downside risks

  • Rates stay higher for longer — the single largest near-term risk, already demonstrated: FFO beat + guidance optics + 10-year >5% = −3.8%. REIT multiples are long-duration assets; a 5.5% 10-year compresses P/FFO toward the bear case regardless of operations.
  • Trade / tariffs hit goods flows — logistics demand is derived demand for moving goods. Tariff-driven import contraction or a consumer slowdown cuts leasing velocity (watch the 60m sq ft quarterly run-rate) before it cuts occupancy.
  • SEGRO integration — a ~$18.8B cross-border combination closing H1 2027: leverage, currency, management bandwidth and synergy delivery are all unproven until reported. Deal value is as reported pre-close; final terms in filings govern.
  • Mark-to-market exhaustion — the ~16% cushion is a wasting asset if market rents fall; a rent downturn would flip rollover from uplift to drag by 2027–28.
  • Data-centre execution — 5.8 GW secured/advanced is a pipeline, not income. Power delivery delays or a decision to self-build at peak construction costs would raise capex intensity and dilute the FFO story.
  • GAAP-guide confusion, recurring — the net-earnings cut ($4.05–$4.15) moved the stock more than the FFO beat. Headline algorithms and casual holders price the wrong line; expect repeated volatility on the same confusion.
What would break the thesis

Two consecutive quarters of occupancy slipping below ~94% with leasing volume materially under the 60m sq ft Q3 pace. That combination would mean the burn-off story is being overtaken by a demand downturn — at which point the bear-case multiple (17×) on slowing FFO, not the base case, is the right frame, and the position should be cut per §6.

PLD — AI trade positioning

Role: Beneficiary. Prologis is the world's largest logistics landlord and is now pushing into data centres, with a 5.8 GW data-centre power pipeline secured or advanced at 30 Sep 2026 (pack) alongside its 1.3B sq ft logistics portfolio.

While the AI trade runs — demand channel: AI demand adds a second engine: data-centre conversions and power-secured development on top of logistics rent growth from e-commerce and the ~16% embedded mark-to-market, while Amazon (5.6% of rent) links logistics demand to AI-funded hyperscaler spending.

While the AI trade runs — multiple channel: Prologis trades at 18.80x P/FFO per pack — a full but not extreme logistics multiple — and the data-centre pipeline supports holding it while conversions lease up, rather than pricing in a speculative AI premium.

When the AI trade unwinds — what breaks, in order: First, demand: data-centre conversions stall if power delivery or hyperscaler leasing slows, and logistics demand softens if trade/tariffs hit goods flows (the pack's stated risk). Second, multiple compression: the data-centre growth premium unwinds first, back toward a pure logistics multiple, with the 10-year above 5% already pressuring the shares. Third, balance sheet: moderate — the SEGRO combination (~$18.8B, approved Sep 2026, expected H1 2027) adds integration and funding demands just as development spending rises. No quantified sensitivity is claimed.

Trigger(s) to watch: Data-centre conversion leasing and power delivery milestones; the SEGRO close in H1 2027; and goods-flow/trade data affecting logistics occupancy (95.6% in Q3).
