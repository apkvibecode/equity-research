01

Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Digital Realty operates as a global data-centre REIT under its PlatformDIGITAL® footprint: it owns and develops data centres and leases powered space and interconnection to hyperscalers, enterprises and service providers. Revenue is fundamentally rent-like and contractual — base rent plus power/interconnection — with growth coming from (a) leasing the existing estate, (b) converting a signed backlog into commenced rent, and (c) delivering new development capacity into demand that, at present, exceeds powered supply.

Core revenue mechanics

  • Colocation & hyperscale leasing: long-term leases of space and power. Q3 revenue grew +20.6% y/y (MarketBeat summary) — a wholesale-led acceleration consistent with AI / hyperscale commencements, not with mature single-digit rent growth.
  • Same-capital cash NOI: guided to 5.25–5.75% growth for FY26 (raised on the Q3 call) — this is the like-for-like rent engine underneath the development story, and at 5%+ it is healthy for an existing estate.
  • Development as a revenue factory: the pipeline is 63% pre-leased at ~11.6% stabilised yield — building to a double-digit yield on cost, pre-sold, is the highest-quality growth a REIT can show, provided power and execution hold (see §4).
  • Honest gap — no segment % split is presented: the verified pack does not break out revenue by product or by region as percentages, so this note does not invent one. The footprint counts above are verified; any finer split should be taken from the latest 10-Q before underwriting beyond the §6 weight.

Structural pivots & dependencies

  • Pivot — from landlord to AI-capacity developer: the centre of gravity has shifted from stabilised rent collection toward backlog conversion and pre-leased development. The earnings mix is therefore becoming more growth-like and more execution-sensitive at the same time.
  • Supply-chain dependency #1 — power: in constrained markets, grid access and power pricing gate both new development and renewal economics. The pack names power-constrained markets as a headline risk — for DLR, power is the supply chain.
  • Supply-chain dependency #2 — hyperscaler demand: the largest tenants drive the backlog. That is demand quality (investment-grade, long leases) and concentration risk in the same line item.
  • Development execution: delivering 63%-pre-leased capacity on schedule and on budget converts the 11.6% stabilised yield from a promise into FFO. Slippage pushes FFO growth from the §5 base path toward the bear path without any change in demand.
Reading DLR correctly

Do not anchor on the Q3 GAAP EPS miss ($0.48 vs $0.52). For a data-centre REIT, GAAP EPS subtracts depreciation on assets that are leasing up at record rates — it can fall while the business strengthens. The operating truth of Q3 is in core FFO $2.10 (+11.1%), the guidance raise to $8.30–$8.35, and the record backlog. Judge DLR on FFO conversion, the way §2 and §5 do.

Balance-sheet diagnostics — what can and cannot be said

  • What the pack verifies: price, market cap, yield, FFO path, guidance, NOI guidance, backlog and pipeline economics above. Those are the figures carried in this note, with sources at the point of use.
  • What the pack does not verify: a line-by-line debt, cash, leverage (net debt / EBITDA) or debt-to-equity breakdown for DLR was not in the verified pack, so this note does not publish one rather than invent numbers. For a development-heavy REIT this is a genuine open diligence item: check the latest 10-Q for total debt, maturity ladder, rate mix and development funding commitments before sizing beyond the §6 weight.
  • FFO quality check that is available: guidance was raised mid-cycle ($8.15–$8.20 → $8.30–$8.35) on both FFO and same-capital cash NOI (to 5.25–5.75%) — raises on both the development-inclusive and like-for-like lines suggest breadth, not a single-lease fluke. The verification log confirms both raises independently via Zacks.
  • Capital-allocation read: a 2.71% yield alongside a 63%-pre-leased development programme signals a balanced growth REIT: the dividend is funded while capital is recycled into ~11.6%-yield development. The risk in that model is funding cost — if capital markets price REIT debt wider, the spread between development yield and funding cost compresses, and the §5 bull path (24×) becomes hard to defend.
The spread is the story

DLR's equity case is, arithmetically, a spread trade: build at ~11.6% stabilised yield, fund at REIT capital costs, and let the market capitalise the resulting FFO at ~21×. Every leg is observable — pre-leasing (63%), FFO delivery ($2.10 and rising guidance), and the exit multiple. If any leg breaks — power delays, execution slippage, or multiple compression toward the bear-case 18× — the §5 bear outcomes ($155 near-term, $191 over five years) require no crisis, only normal friction.

03

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Moat rating: wide on scale and relationships; narrower than EQIX on interconnection density. The pack's moat statement is: global footprint + hyperscaler relationships, development pipeline. DLR's advantage is being the counterparty hyperscalers call when they need capacity in many countries at once, delivered by a developer with a pre-leased track record.

Moat strengths

  • Global footprint at count scale: 300+ centres across 6 regions — a multinational or hyperscaler can expand with one landlord across continents, a procurement and consistency advantage regional rivals cannot match.
  • Hyperscaler relationships: the record $2.5B backlog ($1.85B at share) is itself evidence — hyperscalers do not pre-commit at that scale to a developer they expect to fail on delivery.
  • Development pipeline as a moat-in-progress: 63% pre-leased at ~11.6% stabilised yield means future capacity is substantially sold before it is built. Securing powered land and pre-leasing it compounds the footprint advantage.
  • Tenant breadth: 5,000+ customers diversify the base beneath the hyperscale headline — enterprise and service-provider tenancy stabilises occupancy through cloud capex cycles.
  • Pricing signal: same-capital cash NOI guided to 5.25–5.75% growth indicates genuine rent pricing power on the existing estate, not growth bought solely with new capital.

Competitors & moat limits

  • Equinix (EQIX): the direct listed rival — for context from the same verified sector pack, EQIX is #3 by market cap ($100.34B) with the denser interconnection ecosystem. DLR wins on wholesale scale; EQIX wins on network density. They compete hardest for enterprise hybrid-cloud demand.
  • Hyperscaler self-build: the largest customers are also the best-capitalised potential competitors. Every hyperscaler-owned campus is wholesale demand that never reaches DLR's backlog.
  • Power owners as kingmakers: where powered land is the binding constraint, whoever controls power wins the next development regardless of brand. The moat protects the existing 300+ estate more reliably than it guarantees the next site.
  • Private platforms: private data-centre developers compete for the same hyperscale leases without public disclosure — competitive intensity for each large deal is partly invisible from outside.
  • Customer concentration: a backlog built on large hyperscale leases cuts both ways — a single tenant's capex pause can move commencements, and therefore FFO, by a visible amount.

Positioning verdict: DLR is the scale-and-development leader in listed data centres, ranked #6 in the VNQ-universe top 10 by market cap ($67.04B). The moat justifies a premium P/FFO versus generic REITs; at 21.7× current FFO the premium is moderate rather than heroic — §5's bull case (24×) is roughly the ceiling the moat alone defends without faster FFO growth, while the base case needs no expansion at all (21×).

04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts

  • AI leasing commencements accelerating over the next 2 quarters: stated on the Q3 call and the pack's lead catalyst. Signed backlog only becomes FFO at commencement — an acceleration converts the $2.5B backlog from a promise into the FY26–27 FFO line.
  • Backlog conversion at scale: $2.5B at 100% share ($1.85B at DLR share) is a record book. Each tranche that commences on schedule de-risks the ~13% guided FFO growth and supports the §5 base growth path (9%).
  • Guidance momentum: FY26 FFO and NOI guidance were both raised at Q3. A further raise — or early FY27 guidance at double-digit FFO growth — would validate the bull-case growth assumption (12%).
  • Pre-leased deliveries landing at ~11.6% yield: each stabilised delivery adds high-yield NOI without leasing risk on 63% of the pipeline, compounding FFO per share if share count and funding are managed.

Downside risks

  • Power-constrained markets: the pack's lead risk. Grid delays or power-cost spikes stall commencements and new development alike — and unlike a leasing slowdown, management cannot market its way around a missing megawatt.
  • Development execution: construction cost, timing and lease-up on the un-leased 37% of the pipeline. The 11.6% stabilised yield is an underwriting figure until assets stabilise.
  • Customer concentration: hyperscaler-weighted backlog means tenant capex decisions transmit quickly into DLR's commencement schedule; insourcing (self-build) is the slow version of the same risk.
  • Rate & multiple sensitivity (REIT structural): at 21.7× FFO there is less premium to lose than at EQIX's ~26.5× AFFO, but the §5 bear case still only needs an 18× exit multiple — a normal higher-for-longer de-rating — to cap five-year returns at ~+6% total.
  • Funding-cost spread compression: if REIT debt costs rise toward development yields, new pre-leased growth adds less FFO per dollar of capital, pressuring both growth and the dividend's headroom (yield 2.71% today).
What would break the thesis

Commencements slipping while the backlog stays signed — revenue deferred, not lost, quarter after quarter — alongside FFO growth sliding toward the bear-case 5%. Either alone is a timing issue the balance sheet can likely absorb; together they would mean the AI-capacity conversion story is stalling, and at 21.7× FFO the multiple would do the near-term damage (bear $155) before earnings do.

DLR — AI trade positioning

Role: Enabler. Digital Realty operates 300+ data centres for 5,000+ customers and is the wholesale hyperscale landlord: its Q3 2026 print showed a $2.5B backlog, a pipeline 63% pre-leased at ~11.6% stabilised yield, and AI leasing commencements accelerating (pack).

While the AI trade runs — demand channel: Hyperscaler AI demand converts directly into leased megawatts, backlog conversion and renewal pricing — above-1 MW hyperscale renewals repriced 66.7% higher on a cash basis in recent reporting — making AI the primary demand engine.

While the AI trade runs — multiple channel: DLR trades on AI growth expectations (the real anchor is 21x core FFO in the pack's 5-yr model), so the multiple channel is the AI premium itself: while AI leasing accelerates, the market capitalises the backlog and pipeline.

When the AI trade unwinds — what breaks, in order: First, demand: hyperscalers delay or cancel AI builds, or shift to self-build, leaving the 63%-pre-leased pipeline and the $2.5B backlog to slip — the largest single customer is 10.8% of annualised recurring revenue, so each signature matters. Second, multiple compression: from an AI-growth multiple toward a through-cycle data-centre multiple, with Q3 promote income flattering the run-rate. Third, balance sheet: development is capital-hungry (pipeline cost ~$20B per reporting) and power-constrained markets are the bottleneck, so a demand pause strands development capital. No quantified sensitivity is claimed.

Trigger(s) to watch: Hyperscaler AI capex guidance and lease commencements; backlog conversion versus the $2.5B figure; and power availability in constrained markets.
