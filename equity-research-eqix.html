01

Business Model & Segment Breakdown

Equinix operates as a global colocation and interconnection platform structured as a REIT. Customers rent cabinets, power and — critically — connections inside facilities where the major clouds, networks and enterprises are already present. Revenue is therefore best understood as recurring rent-like colocation income plus interconnection income that grows denser, and stickier, with every additional participant in a metro ecosystem.

Core revenue streams

  • Colocation: space, power and cooling sold by the cabinet / cage / suite. This is the rent engine — long-lived, contractual, and priced on scarce powered capacity in constrained metros.
  • Interconnection (Equinix Fabric): virtual and physical connections between customers, clouds and carriers inside the platform. The pack is explicit: interconnection is the moat product, not just space/power. It carries higher strategic value per unit than raw space because it is where switching costs live.
  • Adjacent platform services: services that ride on the same footprint and customer relationships. The pack does not break out a separate revenue share for these — no segment % is stated here because none was verified in the pack; inventing a split would misstate the business.

Geographic & structural notes

  • Global, metro-led: 70+ metros means exposure is diversified by market, but value concentrates where network density and cloud on-ramps coincide. The pack does not provide a verified regional revenue split for EQIX, so none is presented — the honest gap is noted rather than filled.
  • Structural pivot — AI readiness: the estate is marketed as AI-ready, aligning the colocation base to AI inference and enterprise hybrid-cloud demand (the catalyst in §4), plus the Nvidia private-cloud partnership cited in the pack.
  • Supply-chain dependency #1 — power: new capacity is gated by power availability and pricing (see §4). For a data-centre REIT, grid connection and power cost are the supply chain.
  • Supply-chain dependency #2 — hyperscaler behaviour: the largest customers can also build for themselves. Customer concentration at the ecosystem level is a feature for density and a risk for bargaining power.
Reading EQIX correctly

If you model Equinix as generic square footage, you will undervalue the interconnection density and overvalue any single new building. The unit of value is the metro ecosystem: each additional cloud, carrier and enterprise in a facility raises the value of every other tenancy in it. That is why §3 scores the moat on network effects first and real estate second.

Trailing P/E — 60.59× for EQIX [Finnhub] — is inflated by the heavy depreciation REITs charge against real estate that often holds or gains value, and the research pack instructs explicitly that it must NOT be used for valuation. Balance-sheet diagnostics — what can and cannot be said

  • What the pack verifies: market cap, price, yield, revenue TTM and the AFFO path above. Those are the figures carried in this note with sources.
  • What the pack does not verify: a line-by-line debt, cash, leverage (net debt / EBITDA) or debt-to-equity breakdown for EQIX was not in the verified pack, so this note does not publish one rather than invent numbers. For a REIT, treat that as an open diligence item: check the latest 10-Q for total debt, weighted maturity and rate mix before sizing beyond the §6 weight.
  • Structural point that does not need a ratio: data-centre REITs fund growth with continuous capital — new capacity is power-hungry and capex-intensive — so AFFO (which deducts recurring capex from FFO) is the honest earnings proxy, and it is the basis of both the dividend capacity and the §5 valuation. If AFFO growth stalls, both the income and the multiple compress together.
  • Capital allocation read: a 2.04% yield alongside an AFFO-growth story signals a growth-first REIT: cash is retained and raised to build interconnection-dense capacity, with the dividend as a discipline device rather than the main return channel. That suits the risk-8/10 profile in §6 only at a satellite weight.
The premium is the risk

At 26.5× current AFFO, EQIX is priced for the base-to-bull AFFO path in §5. There is no multiple cushion: the bear case in §5 does not require a business collapse — only AFFO growth slowing to 5% and the market paying 22× for it — and it still produces $1,074 over five years (barely above today's price) and $850 over 12–18 months. Position size, not price hope, is the control for that asymmetry.

03

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Moat rating: wide — network-effects interconnection. The pack's moat statement is short and load-bearing: clouds and carriers colocate where each other are; switching costs are high. That is a classic two-sided density moat applied to physical infrastructure.

Moat strengths

  • Network effects: every additional participant in a facility raises the value of being in that facility. A rival can build a building; it cannot quickly replicate 10,500+ customers' worth of counterparties in 70+ metros.
  • Switching costs: moving an interconnected deployment means re-wiring business relationships, not just moving servers. Interconnection (Equinix Fabric) embeds EQIX in the customer's network architecture.
  • Hyperscalers as magnets: Amazon, Apple and Meta as customers anchor ecosystems that pull in enterprises who want low-latency access to those platforms — the biggest names validate the locations for everyone else.
  • Global consistency: 280+ centres / 70+ metros let a multinational buy one standard in every market — a procurement advantage single-market rivals cannot match.
  • AI-ready estate: positioning the installed base for AI inference and hybrid cloud puts the moat in the path of the current demand wave rather than behind it.

Competitors & moat limits

  • Digital Realty (DLR): the direct listed rival at scale. For context from the same verified sector pack: DLR Q3 core FFO was $2.10 (+11.1%), FY26 core FFO guidance was raised to $8.30–$8.35, and backlog was a record $2.5B at 100% share ($1.85B at DLR share) — DLR is executing, not standing still.
  • Hyperscaler self-build: the largest customers are also the best-capitalised potential competitors. Every hyperscaler campus built for own use is demand that never reaches the colocation market.
  • CyrusOne (private) and other private platforms compete for the same enterprise and cloud workloads without public-market disclosure — competitive intensity is partly invisible.
  • Power as a leveller: where powered land is the binding constraint, whoever secures power wins the next building regardless of interconnection heritage. The moat protects existing ecosystems more than it guarantees the next site.
  • Pricing power is real but bounded: interconnection supports premium pricing inside dense metros; commodity space/power outside them is contestable and price-sensitive.

Positioning verdict: EQIX is the interconnection leader within a sector where the #3-by-market-cap slot ($100.34B) already reflects that leadership. The moat justifies a premium P/AFFO versus generic REITs; it does not justify any P/AFFO — §5's bull case (30×) is approximately the ceiling the moat alone can defend without faster AFFO growth.

04

Catalysts & Downside Risks

Catalysts

  • AI inference & enterprise hybrid-cloud demand: the pack's lead catalyst. Inference workloads are latency-sensitive and distributed — exactly the shape that favours interconnected metro facilities over remote mega-campuses.
  • Nvidia private-cloud partnership: cited in the pack as a named catalyst — a channel for enterprises to deploy private AI infrastructure inside EQIX ecosystems, which would deepen both colocation and interconnection revenue per customer.
  • AFFO delivery vs. the +11.12% estimate: if reported AFFO tracks the $42.48 consensus path, the forward P/AFFO compresses from 26.5× to ~23.9× on price alone — the de-rating happens through earnings, the healthy way.
  • Power-secured capacity coming online: in a power-constrained market, each powered, interconnected expansion delivered into scarcity supports both occupancy pricing and interconnection attach.

Downside risks

  • Power availability & pricing for new builds: the pack's lead risk. Delays or cost spikes in securing power directly delay revenue and raise the capex per unit of AFFO.
  • Hyperscaler insourcing: self-build by the largest customers removes the highest-volume demand from the addressable market and weakens the magnet effect over time.
  • Valuation risk — ~60× trailing P/E headline / 26.5× P/AFFO: the pack flags the 60× trailing P/E explicitly. Even on the correct AFFO basis the multiple leaves no room for an AFFO miss (see §2 callout).
  • Data-centre power politics: the same load debate flagged for Utilities — grid, community and regulatory pushback on large power loads can slow approvals and raise costs across the sector, EQIX included.
  • Rate sensitivity (REIT structural): as a REIT trading on a cash-flow multiple, EQIX's exit P/AFFO is sensitive to long rates; a higher-for-longer regime pressures the multiple in §5's bear case (22×) even if operations hold.
What would break the thesis

Evidence that interconnection density is decoupling from value: AFFO growth slowing toward the bear-case 5% while hyperscalers visibly route new AI capacity to self-built sites. Either alone is a slowdown; together they would mean the network-effects moat is no longer compounding — and at 26.5× AFFO, the exit multiple, not just earnings, would do the damage.

EQIX — AI trade positioning

Role: Enabler. Equinix operates 280+ AI-ready data centres across 70+ metros with 10,500+ customers, and its moat product is interconnection (Equinix Fabric) — the network effect that makes AI and hybrid-cloud workloads colocate where networks and clouds already are.

While the AI trade runs — demand channel: AI inference and enterprise hybrid-cloud demand drives colocation, power and interconnection revenue, with the Nvidia private-cloud partnership (pack) as a direct AI workload channel.

While the AI trade runs — multiple channel: Equinix trades at a premium data-centre multiple (60.59x trailing P/E, AFFO growth +11.12% expected per pack), so the AI boom is already capitalised — the multiple channel is continued support while AI workloads grow, not fresh re-rating.

When the AI trade unwinds — what breaks, in order: First, demand: hyperscalers insource AI capacity or delay enterprise AI deployments, slowing colocation and interconnection growth; diversification across 10,500+ customers cushions single-tenant loss but not a broad AI pause. Second, multiple compression: from a premium multiple toward a through-cycle data-centre multiple if AI growth disappoints — the shares have already drifted lower since the June-quarter guidance raise per reporting. Third, balance sheet: development for AI-ready capacity is power- and capital-intensive, and power availability/pricing for new builds (pack risk) is the bottleneck; leverage is material at $24.05B net debt. No quantified sensitivity is claimed.

Trigger(s) to watch: Hyperscaler self-build announcements; enterprise AI deployment and interconnection growth data; and power availability/pricing for new builds in constrained metros.
