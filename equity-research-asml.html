01 · Business model

Business Model & Segment Breakdown

ASML sells lithography systems — EUV (extreme ultraviolet) for the most advanced nodes and DUV for mature nodes — plus a growing installed-base service business. It is the only EUV supplier in the world; DUV rivals Nikon and Canon do not compete in EUV.

Geography & customers: Netherlands HQ; customers concentrated in Taiwan, Korea, the US and China — effectively TSMC, Samsung and Intel at the leading edge. Supply-chain dependency: decades of optics and mechatronics IP (notably precision optics) and a vast service installed base. 03 · Moat

Competitive Moat & Industry Positioning

Why the moat is real

  • Sole EUV supplier — no direct competitor exists
  • Decades of optics / mechatronics IP that cannot be replicated quickly
  • Service installed base creates recurring, high-margin revenue and lock-in
  • Every leading-edge roadmap (2nm, high-NA) runs through ASML tools

Limits to the moat

  • Regulatory advantage cuts both ways — export controls can remove its China market
  • Extreme customer concentration: three buyers dominate leading-edge orders
  • DUV is contestable (Nikon / Canon), EUV is not

Pricing power: Demonstrated — 57.5% gross margin at scale, with demand exceeding planned supply for 2027–28.

ASML — AI trade positioning

Role: Enabler — the monopoly bottleneck. ASML is the sole EUV supplier: Q3 2026 net sales of €11.7B, net income ~€5.0B, 57.5% gross margin, FY2026 guide €44–45B, and the pack cites its statement that planned 2027–28 supply will not fully meet demand.

While the AI trade runs, demand comes through EUV and high-NA orders from TSMC, Samsung and Intel, with backlog — not quarterly sales — as the pack's leading indicator. On multiples, ASML trades around 40x central trailing earnings in the pack (vendor range 38.6–55.2x), a premium that assumes the 2nm/high-NA ramp converts backlog into shipments.

If the AI trade unwinds, demand breaks first through fab capex cuts and order push-outs — the pack notes some customers are already seeking 2029–30 slots, which cuts both ways — with DUV demand falling before EUV. Multiple compression follows toward a through-cycle equipment multiple, and the Q3 cash surge (customer deposits) reverses, which is why this build uses FY2025 FCF rather than annualising Q3. The balance sheet is strong (€13.32B cash and short-term investments against €4.39B debt at FY2025-end). Triggers to watch: order intake/backlog commentary, China export-licence decisions, TSMC/Intel/Samsung capex guides, and the 27 Jan 2027 results.
