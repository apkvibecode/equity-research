01

Business model & segment breakdown

What it sells, and where

T-Mobile US is a pure-play US wireless carrier: consumer and business mobile service, plus fixed wireless broadband delivered over its 5G network. Its footprint is the United States, including Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands — there is no international segment to dilute or complicate the story.

Core revenue streams

  • Consumer wireless — postpaid and prepaid mobile plans, the historic core of the business.
  • Business wireless — enterprise and government accounts, an explicit growth focus in the pack.
  • Fixed wireless broadband — home internet over 5G, taking share from cable without laying last-mile cable.

Structural pivots

  • 5G leadership from the Sprint merger — the acquired 2.5 GHz spectrum is the foundation of the current network advantage.
  • Fibre JV expansion — moving into fibre via joint ventures, extending beyond wireless-only access.
  • Capital return matures — the story has shifted from build-out spending to a dividend plus buybacks (Section 2).
Estimate — not re-verified: the pack puts revenue at roughly ~$84B with margins expanding, and uses $84B as the 2026E base in the valuation model. That figure was not independently re-verified, so treat it — and everything modelled from it — as an estimate, not a confirmed actual. Q3 2026 results were released on 28 Oct (per MarketBeat).

Dependencies to keep in view: spectrum is the raw material — future spectrum costs and availability directly affect the cost base — and Deutsche Telekom's controlling stake (flagged again in Risks) means strategic direction is not set by US public shareholders alone.

03

Competitive moat & industry positioning

Why customers stay

The moat is physical: spectrum. T-Mobile's 2.5 GHz holdings from the Sprint merger give it the deepest mid-band 5G capacity of the US majors — the layer that delivers both speed and coverage — and spectrum, once licensed, cannot be replicated by spending alone on any quick timetable.

Moat evidence in the pack

  • Best 5G spectrum depth (2.5 GHz) among the majors.
  • Lowest churn among the majors recently — customers leaving at the slowest rate is the market's own verdict on network quality.
  • Scale — at $165.7B it is large enough to fund network and fibre investment from its own cash generation.

Competitive set

  • Verizon (VZ) and AT&T (T) — the other two national carriers; the direct fight for postpaid subscribers.
  • Cable operators — competing from the other direction, bundling mobile with broadband and attacking fixed wireless's home-internet pitch.

Pricing power follows from churn: a carrier customers don't leave can hold price and sell up (business accounts, fixed wireless) instead of discounting to defend its base. The vulnerability is symmetrical — if a price war starts (Risks), spectrum depth doesn't prevent margin compression, it only makes T-Mobile the likely survivor of one.

04

Catalysts & downside risks

What moves it next

Catalysts

  • Fibre JV expansion — a second access product alongside fixed wireless.
  • Business-segment growth — enterprise share is the under-penetrated pool.
  • Buyback execution — the 4.39% buyback yield mechanically lifts EPS if delivered.
  • Margin expansion — the pack's base case assumes margins keep expanding from the ~$84B revenue base (estimate).

Downside risks

  • Price war — promotional escalation by VZ, T or cable compresses the whole industry's pricing.
  • Integration / fibre execution — JV fibre is a new muscle; delays or cost overruns hit the pivot story.
  • Deutsche Telekom control — a controlling parent can act in its own interest over minority holders'.
  • Spectrum costs — future auctions or renewals are large, lumpy cash demands.

Read-through. AI trade positioning — TMUS

Role: Low exposure. T-Mobile's AI exposure is network and customer-experience efficiency, not AI infrastructure: the pack's thesis is 5G leadership, fibre JVs and buybacks, with FCF of $18.65bn TTM funding returns.

While the AI trade runs. While the AI trade runs, demand grows through business 5G and fixed-wireless as enterprises digitise; the multiple is supported by margin expansion and buybacks, not an AI premium.

When the AI trade unwinds. If the trade unwinds, demand breaks first in business growth and fibre-JV execution if enterprise spending stalls; then the multiple compresses toward a through-cycle telecom multiple; then the balance sheet — debt $118.0bn against cash $5.6bn — limits buyback pace. Watch: postpaid adds, fibre execution, and any price war with Verizon/AT&T or a fourth-carrier entrant.
